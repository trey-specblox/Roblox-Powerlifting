# Meet Study

The main reference from the study of real powerlifting meet videos (November 2026), written for the Roblox powerlifting game (cartoon R15 lifters in six builds; meets from Local up to State, Nationals, Worlds and Sheffield-style shows).

- Who it is for: an animator or game designer who has NOT seen the videos, and the project owner (a beginner).
- Celebrations are summarised here as rules and patterns. Every single celebration, move by move, is in [[Meet Celebration Catalogue]].
- Raw notes (one file per batch): [[Meet notes A1]], [[Meet notes A2]], [[Meet notes A3]], [[Meet notes A4]], [[Meet notes A5]], [[Meet notes A6]], [[Meet notes S1]], [[Meet notes S2]], [[Meet notes C1]], [[Meet notes C2]], [[Meet notes C3]], [[Meet notes C4]], [[Meet notes X1]].
- How to read source references: `video id m:ss` (for the 4-hour broadcast `eu_9nPA3zRs` the times are `mmm:ss`, e.g. 203:05 = 3 h 23 min 05 s). "E1-E5" is the energy scale the note-takers used (1 = nothing or a smile, 5 = jumping, roaring, everything).
- "Disagreement" boxes mark places where two batches saw things differently.

## Key facts

1. 38 videos (~8 h 50 min, ~500 meet attempts) were gridded at 1 frame per second, every second, muted; small 480p pictures limit fine detail (thumbs, faces).
2. One attempt is ~40-60 s of broadcast; lifters have a 1-minute clock and use most of it.
3. Setups are LONG and still: squat head bowed under the bar 8-15 s (up to 25 s), braced 4-11 s before "squat"; bench 5-18 s lying; deadlift wedge 5-20 s with hips high until the last ~0.5 s.
4. The lifts themselves are short: squat ~1-1.5 s down / 1-2 s up (grinders 3-8 s); bench ~1 s down, 0.5-1.5 s pause, 1-2 s up; deadlift 0.5-1.5 s (grinders 2.5-6 s); holds 1-4 s.
5. Squat spotters crouch with EVERY rep, hands cupped under the bar ends; bench side spotters follow the sleeves; three of them save a missed bench together.
6. The head referee's bench "start" is a visible flat arm held out ~2 s (up to 9 s) and dropped.
7. Most lifters do not stand and stare at the lights; the venue shows the result (LED "GOOD LIFT", green/red light washes, purple light for record attempts).
8. About half of good lifts get any gesture; openers get nothing; big celebrations are a few per session and come with 3rd attempts, PRs, records, upsets, wins and grinders.
9. Personality beats weight: the biggest lifts (430 kg, 485 kg, the all-time total record, open world records) often got the smallest reactions.
10. Celebrations happen WHILE WALKING OFF (one short beat at the bar), toward the crowd or backstage, and almost every attempt ends in a coach hug or hand clasp at the exit.
11. Each lifter repeats one or two signature rituals every attempt (ear hold, wall lean, fist bumps with every loader, ponytail fix, prayer-pose under the bar).
12. Coach rituals are backstage and short: trap kneading 10-20 s, ear holds 6-12 s, ammonia ~3 s, slaps ~3 s, headphones lifted off at the last moment.
13. Misses are many different bodies (stall, bail, dump, faint, fall forward/backward, grip failure, lockout stall) but the reactions are mostly calm; big reactions come at elimination.
14. Deadlifts are lowered with both hands after "down"; no good lift was ever dropped from the top.
15. Lever belts are worn low; tightened at the bar (the most common deadlift ritual), often unbuckled walking off; ripped off and swung overhead only rarely; the one-hand take-off flick is a ~0.3 s lever pop.
16. Venues differ hugely by meet type: dark LED theatre (Avancus, Sheffield), bright plain white-and-blue hall (IPF Worlds), warehouse hall (Nationals), hotel ballroom.
17. Batches disagree on whether celebrations start before or after the lights; the safe rule is: clear lift = react at once, close lift = stand still and wait, then explode.

Coverage: every second of every video was gridded at 1 frame per second; every batch reports "nothing skipped" (gaps from ads, hidden panes or tab losses were re-gridded). Two short genuine black stretches are in the videos themselves (3i4jvmDrtdY 0:27-0:31; LxSRpjl_UGU 8:15-8:20).

### 1.2 Method

- Every second of every video was gridded at 1 frame per second (16 frames per sheet, sheets back to back, no gaps). Any sheet that came back broken (an ad, the YouTube page instead of the grid, a hidden pane) was redrawn, so no second was skipped.
- Close-ups at 0.25-0.5 s steps (cropped and enlarged, brightened where the stage was dark) were made through every attempt's setup, lift, lights, walk-off and every reaction or ritual.
- The commentary was read from captions or the auto-transcript wherever one existed, to get weights, lights and context.
- Mid-roll ads interrupted the Avancus broadcast many times (8 times in one 40-minute block alone, [[Meet notes A5]]); each time the same grid was redrawn after the ad.

### 1.3 Limits (read before trusting a detail)

- **Muted.** Nothing was listened to. Sound comes only from caption tags such as [cheering] and [screaming], from commentary text, and from open mouths. Commands ("squat", "start", "press", "rack", "down") were never heard; their timing is inferred from body pauses and referee arm gestures ([[Meet notes S2]]: "commands never audible").
- **Small pictures.** The browser pane returned screenshots about 800 px wide, so a 1-s grid frame is only about 190-200 px wide. Faces, thumbs and grip type are often unreadable in grids and come from close-ups only.
- **480p.** The Sheffield videos (S1, S2 V1-V4) were only offered at 480p; one Nationals video (S2 V6) is a single far camera where the lifter is about 60 px tall, so faces and fingers are not readable there.
- **Dark stages.** The Avancus stage is very dark (the live chat complained); grids from 20:48 on were brightened x1.8 ([[Meet notes A1]]).
- **Lights often off-screen.** Physical referee lights are almost never in shot. The Avancus broadcast shows lights only as three small circles in the graphics; Sheffield adds a floor light box glimpsed at the stage front. Who looks at the lights, and for how long, is read from head turns.
- **Edits.** Single-lifter edits (Sheffield, Worlds) cut out loading, other lifters and some walk-ons. Two clips appear twice in S1 (the 251 kg bench and the 356 kg squat); they were counted once.

## 4. Walk-on, crowd hype, signature rituals and the belt

### 4.1 Walk-on

- Pace: normal walking pace, unhurried, arms loose or slightly away from the body (big lats), eyes on the bar or straight ahead; hands on hips or on the belt is common (eu_9nPA3zRs 55:10-55:14, 183:42, 184:42, 192:50). No strut for most. Walk-on through the tunnel takes ~8-12 s (A6).

### 4.5 The belt

All belts seen are LEVER belts (a wide band ~10 cm tall with a black steel lever plate at the front), worn LOW: bottom edge at the top of the shorts, from just below to across the navel (ngP2vaQHlPg). The belt goes through four moments.

**1. Putting it on (the put-on flick, ngP2vaQHlPg 0.00-0.95 s, frame by frame)**
- 0.00-0.10: belt open, held in the LEFT hand at the left hip, free end hanging by the left thigh; right hand on the right hip.
- 0.15: right hand reaches across low.
- 0.20: one quick wrist flick; the belt swings round the front and the lever end swings up from the left into the right hand (the swing itself ~0.1 s).
- 0.25-0.30: belt stretched across the lower belly, one end in each hand at the hips.
- 0.40-0.70: both hands bring the ends together at the navel.
- 0.80-0.90: head down, looking at the buckle, both hands centred, pulling tight. Whole move ~0.9 s.
- 1.00-1.75: both hands at the lever, the right hand gives the tail a short tug down, the lever is closed.

**2. Closing and cinching at the meet**
- Most lifters arrive with the belt already done up backstage: both hands on the lever (eu_9nPA3zRs 50:53-51:03), often the coach cinches it from behind with his arms round the lifter, or from the front (166:01-166:05, 184:36-184:38.5, 188:20-188:22).
- On the platform the belt is yanked tighter with both hands, elbows out, leaning forward: the single most common pre-lift move at the deadlift bar (~12 times in one 40-minute block, [[Meet notes A5]]; cheeks puffed with effort on the meet-winning pull, 179:57-180:01); a forward belt tug is one lifter's squat signature (23:41, 31:33). A few close the lever on the platform: grab, lean, push the lever shut, check it again (64:15.5-64:17.5); or snap it shut ~5 s before a record deadlift (208:49-208:54). Sheffield men re-tighten at the corner with both hands at the navel, elbows out (brWUtR69PFc 1:40-1:45).
- "Belt check" (hands at the buckle a moment just before the pull) was seen on 10+ deadlifts in one Worlds session (oGfa7HoDrrA 0:25-0:28 ... 11:33-11:41).

**3. Taking it off (the take-off flick, ngP2vaQHlPg 9.00-14.50, eight times, all with the RIGHT hand)**
- Right hand to the lever (fingers or thumb under its edge) 0.1-0.2 s, the other hand relaxed on the hip.
- One sharp outward-and-forward YANK (a single blur frame, ~0.1 s) pops the lever open.
- The whole belt flies off the front of the body to the yanking-hand side (0.1-0.2 s).
- It hangs open from the right hand by the right thigh and keeps swinging 1-2 times as the lifter walks away. Pop to belt-in-hand: ~0.2-0.4 s. Two of the eight were done while walking toward the camera (12.60, 13.20).
- The sound would be a sharp lever pop or snap, not a leather creak (no audio was used; this matches the project's belt-snap note).

**4. After the lift at a meet**
- Unbuckling while walking off is common, especially for men after deadlifts: 5 of 8 belted lifters in the men's last deadlift round, good lift or miss ([[Meet notes A6]]); about 6 times in another block ([[Meet notes A5]]); within ~2 s of leaving the bar (brWUtR69PFc 2:28.5-2:33). The belt is then carried by the buckle end in one hand (eu_9nPA3zRs 203:08) or in both hands at the waist (205:10.5). Many keep it on until backstage (lNOrz4CVNMY 1:04-1:10; Sheffield 2025 edits).
- **Belt rip as a celebration** (rare, about 5 times in the whole study): opens the belt behind the bar, rips it off with the right hand and swings it in a big arc over the head, then holds it up in a V (eu_9nPA3zRs 203:03.5-203:08); rips it off and holds it straight up overhead, shaken like a trophy (gfp-IdEaQNc 0:18.5-0:19.8); right arm straight up holding the belt, swung high toward the stage edge (FkQMMNSD6HI 2:11.5-2:14); a woman swings it overhead walking off a record (9FQM0Z6_s8Q 0:42.5-0:43.5).
- **Belt rip in anger** (once): after a failed deadlift the right hand goes straight to the lever, rips it open, the lifter turns and walks off fast with the belt held low behind him (lNOrz4CVNMY 19:57.5-20:00; commentary "he throws his belt off in anger").

> **Disagreement.** [[Meet notes S1]], [[Meet notes S2]] and [[Meet notes C2]] say no belt snaps, flicks or rip-offs were seen in their videos; [[Meet notes A6]], [[Meet notes C1]], [[Meet notes C3]] and [[Meet notes C4]] each recorded one or two. Read this as "rare, a few per meet", not "never". The fast one-hand flick itself was seen only in the gym montage (ngP2vaQHlPg), never at a meet.


## 5. Getting to and holding the bar

### 5.1 Squat

- **Approach.** Avancus lifters walk up to the front of the rack; Sheffield lifters enter from the BACK of the platform, walk between the uprights and step under from behind so they face the crowd (opYQsnkV1Vs 2:36-2:38; [[Meet notes S2]] same for all Sheffield squats).
- **Grip width.** Wide on everyone: hands well outside the shoulders, about 1.5x shoulder width (eu_9nPA3zRs 21:55; brWUtR69PFc ~1.6x, elbows up). Women and small lifters go wider, some nearly at the collars (84 kg woman eu_9nPA3zRs 45:29; HviCculb2Vw 0:22 "very wide, near the collars"). Heavy men also wide (57:22).
- **One hand first.** Right hand first in most clear Avancus shots (41:14, 51:16, 52:04, 53:38, 74:20), the arm straight out, then the left; one lifter left first (31:39). Sheffield lifters put both hands on together (brWUtR69PFc 0:45.0, 9VK3zm60ohg 0:24.5, FwruiLgnapQ 0:21). Worlds: right hand reaches along the bar to near the collar, then the left, then elbows bent back (c5oY-m6ypNk 0:07-0:09).
- **Thumbs.** Not readable at this resolution in most squat shots; where readable (light men at Sheffield and Worlds) the thumbs are AROUND the bar ([[Meet notes C2]]). No batch recorded a thumbless squat grip, but it cannot be ruled out from these pictures.
- **Grip width by build** ([[Meet notes X1]]): super-heavies almost at the collars, ~1.7x shoulder width (A5A39IMP3gs 0:30-0:34); equipped lifters 1.3-1.5x; low-bar 74 kg men just outside the shoulders, elbows up and back, bar below the rear delts (UnUJPzEesc4 4:48, 5:53-5:55); light 59-66 kg men 1.7-2x, hands near the uprights (up-LEMKGRA8 2:16).
- **Standing with the bar at the chest first.** Several Sheffield men stand on the far side gripping the bar at upper-chest height for 2-5 s before ducking under (c6JJZD1W-QA 2:13-2:21).
- **Signature squat approach.** One Worlds lifter hangs from the bar with straight arms and his head below it (~2 s lat stretch), then drops to his RIGHT knee under it, arms up to the bar, head bowed ~2 s ("prayer pose"), then stands and ducks under, every squat (lNOrz4CVNMY 2:08-2:15, 5:06-5:12, 8:38-8:40). A super-heavy "charging bull": hunched, rocking side to side with shoulders flared, a knee-lift stomp, then throws both hands up onto the bar and drives under, stamping his feet (YMrXwUrTpkw 1:40-1:48).
- **"Hands up" pause.** Standing in front of the bar holding it with straight arms 3-5 s, staring ahead or at the hands, before ducking under (eu_9nPA3zRs 50:11-50:16, 59:31-59:34, 69:15-69:19).
- **Duck-under and wedge.** Knees bend, head ducks, one foot forward under the bar (brWUtR69PFc 0:46.0), the bar settles on the upper back; then the lifter stays bent 30-45 deg forward in a quarter squat, HEAD BOWED looking at the floor, elbows back and up, wriggling in. Times: 3-7 s light women's openers; 8-15 s normal (A2); 4-8 s Sheffield; ~15-18 s and ~25 s on the heaviest women (eu_9nPA3zRs 28:35-28:54, 47:47-48:11). Then the head comes up, cheeks puff (big breath), elbows pull back, and the lifter stands it up.
- **Walkout.** Short: 2-3 steps back (left foot back, right foot back and out, small adjust), ~1.5 s (eu_9nPA3zRs 52:08.5-52:10.0; brWUtR69PFc 0:50.0-0:51.0). The lightest lifter stayed almost between the uprights (19:35-19:36). Stance a little wider than the shoulders, toes slightly out.
- **The wait.** 4-11 s standing still with the bar before the descent, breathing, cheeks puffed (19:37-19:48 = 11 s; 46:52-46:56 = 4 s; Sheffield 6-10 s).
- **Spotters.** 4-5 around the bar: one at each end (two each side at heavy weights) plus one directly behind. They crouch WITH the lifter on every rep, hands cupped a few cm under the bar ends, and rise with him or her (eu_9nPA3zRs 25:00-25:03, 28:56-29:00; brWUtR69PFc 1:01-1:03.5; dHn-gvJpzPU 0:45-0:47). One lifter told the rear spotter "hang tight" before getting under (NLGyEXp8nJM 2:18).

### 5.2 Bench

- **Bar check first.** Bend over the racked bar with both hands on it, slide them along to find the ring marks, sometimes look closely at the rings (eu_9nPA3zRs 91:49-91:52; 92:39-92:50 ~10 s). Sheffield men straddle the bench and press both hands down on the bar 3-5 s, head down ("almost universal" there: XUCLctkuP0Y 3:34-3:39; opYQsnkV1Vs 3:46-3:50 wiping/checking the pad). Many talk to the hand-off spotter about the count, with hand gestures (XUCLctkuP0Y 3:22-3:26; 9VK3zm60ohg 4:21-4:29, 5:32-5:41; NLGyEXp8nJM 3:07-3:09 handshake and a pat on the spotter's arm).
- **Sitting down.** Sit on the bench end (feet either side or a deep knee bend at the foot), hands may already be on the bar, lie back, slide up under the bar on the back. Quick lifters roll straight back and grip in ~1.5 s (93:49-93:51). Some sit facing the bar with hands on knees ~3-5 s first (111:08-111:11; 123:13-123:18).
- **Feet and arch.** Women: knees well bent, feet pulled back toward the hips, heels down, big arch; one extreme arch left a big gap under the lower back (100:53-100:57). The LEGS-UP setup: roll back with both legs thrown up, knees to chest, feet off the floor, stay ~5 s setting the upper back and grip, then bring the feet down far behind the knees (95:19-95:27; 112:04-112:06). Men arch less, feet planted wide and flat, slightly behind the knees (116:07-116:08), hips lifted then set down (124:33). Lifters lift the head to look down the body at the feet while placing them (HviCculb2Vw 3:21-3:23).
- **Grip.** Wide, at or just inside the ring marks, well outside the shoulders, on women and men alike; thumbs AROUND the bar in every readable shot ("no thumbless grips seen", [[Meet notes A3]]). Wrist wraps on every lifter. Lifters re-grip once (open and close the hand) before the hand-off (123:25-123:28).
- **Hand-off.** A centre spotter (Avancus: a loader in a black HOUSTON tee) stands behind the head, lifts the bar out, then steps back out of shot before "start"; the two side spotters step back half a step and stand with hands low near the sleeves (118:04-118:09; brWUtR69PFc 4:20-4:25).
- **Referee "start".** See 6.4.

### 5.3 Deadlift

- **Stance mix.** Avancus women and most 93 kg men pulled SUMO (wide, toes out, hands inside the knees); the heaviest men and two women pulled conventional ([[Meet notes A5]]). In the men's final round conventional was the majority (7 of 9, [[Meet notes A6]]). Sheffield: women sumo; 74 kg, 93 kg and super-heavy men conventional, the 105 kg man sumo ([[Meet notes S1]]).
- **Order.** Walk up -> stand over the bar 1-3 s -> look at the feet -> bend and grip -> sit the hips down -> pull the slack out (hips rise slightly, chest up, back flat) -> pull. 6-12 s from the first touch (A6 202:44-202:57).
- **Resets are normal.** Bend, grip or just let the hands hang, then stand back up and re-set (about 6 times in one 40-minute block, [[Meet notes A5]]; 209:04-209:08.5 two-stage setup; brWUtR69PFc 8:24-8:34 stood up, pointed at the bar, re-set with only 6 s left on the clock).
- **Wedge.** Hips low, chest tall, arms long and straight, eyes forward or slightly up; hips bob up and down several times; then a still hold (157:29-157:33, 157:45-157:48). Head neutral-to-down looking 1-2 m ahead, comes up as the bar breaks.
- **Grip.** Hands just outside the shins (conventional) or inside the knees (sumo); one hand then the other (167:18 right hand first) or both within ~1 s. Grip type (hook, mixed) is not readable in grids, and no batch reports it; for the game, follow the project's already-approved grip decisions.
- **Setup length depends on style** ([[Meet notes C4]]): sumo can take 15+ s (hands on, hips high, straight arms 5-10 s while working the knees out, then the hips sink and the slack is pulled for 8-10 s until the bar visibly bends before the plates leave the floor: c6JJZD1W-QA 8:09-8:24, 9:16-9:38); conventional can be ~3 s from stepping in to pulling (Mw95YfGN7Vg 6:53-6:58). Other habits: a slow ~11 s loop behind the bar before every attempt (oGfa7HoDrrA 0:07-0:18, 3:40-3:47); walking away from the bar to re-set (lNOrz4CVNMY 16:39-16:47); crouching behind the bar in the sumo position to "feel" it, then standing (OzNSQQoeHWo 4:22-4:24); facing the wall ~5 s before the biggest lift (YLMRf70ALKo 12:32-12:37).
- **Belt at the bar.** Belts are closed backstage, then yanked tighter at the bar with both hands, elbows out, leaning forward (very common), or the lever is closed at the bar (156:09-156:11.5, ~1.5 s).
- **No spotters behind the lifter** in the deadlift at Avancus; the crew waits at the back-corner tables (A6).

### 6.2 Depth, sticking points and faces

- Squat depth: hip crease clearly below the knee on good lifts; torso leans 30-45 deg forward at the bottom (eu_9nPA3zRs 25:00.5, 64:35-64:40). Depth no-lifts were "just a stitch high" and looked fast and easy (22:05-22:08.5, 59:51-59:58).
- Squat sticking point: just above parallel; hips rise first and the chest dips (NLGyEXp8nJM 1:39-1:41).
- Bench sticking point: mid-range (elbows ~90 deg) or the last inch before lockout (134:52; 141:19).
- Deadlift sticking point: just above the knee; misses stall at mid-shin or below the knee (171:52.3; 207:55.5).
- Faces: cheeks fully puffed (held breath) through the whole squat (50:36-50:37, 67:41-67:43); grinders show an open mouth, teeth, a red face (40:30-40:31, 44:59); deadlift grinders a full grimace, teeth bared, eyes squeezed (209:15-16); a pursed-lip long blow-out exhale walking the bar in after a grind (61:56.5-61:58). Intense brows-down before the pull (163:52-163:53). Smiling before lockout on an easy one (42:52; opYQsnkV1Vs 5:58.5 grin at deadlift lockout).

### 6.3 Holds and lowering

- Squat and bench: 1-2 s still at lockout before "rack".
- Deadlift: 1-4 s at lockout, tall, shoulders back, chin up or head tipped back, mouth open breathing out; many look at the head referee during the hold (168:28-168:29); one looked around left and right with a confident smile (207:02); "holds it for good measure" 4 s (179:10-179:14).
- Head tilts back, face to the ceiling, during the deadlift lockout hold very often (oGfa7HoDrrA 4:02.75-4:04.5, 8:41.75-8:45.5; 9FQM0Z6_s8Q 1:37-1:39, 2:15-2:17.5); some yell at the ceiling during the hold (9FQM0Z6_s8Q 6:46-6:48; lNOrz4CVNMY 20:48-20:50.25). Sheffield holds were long, 2.5-4 s, with the commentator begging for the "down" command (9FQM0Z6_s8Q 1:09.5-1:12).
- The deadlift is lowered WITH the bar after "down": both hands stay on, the lifter squats or hinges down with it, and often stays bent over it ~1 s after it lands. No good lift in the whole study was dropped from the top.

> **Disagreement.** [[Meet notes C3]] says some lifters "just drop it fast" after "down" (oGfa7HoDrrA 4:05, 8:46; 9FQM0Z6_s8Q 6:48.5) and one world-record celebrant lowered it fast to the knees still holding (lNOrz4CVNMY 20:50.75); every other batch says lowering is controlled with hands on. Reconciled: the hands stay on the bar, but the speed of the lowering varies from slow and guided (~1-1.5 s) to a fast guided fall (~0.5 s). Letting go at the top was never seen on a good lift.

### 6.4 Commands and referee gestures (seen, not heard)

- **Squat.** "Squat" ends the braced wait (4-11 s after the walkout). "Rack": the head referee (Sheffield: in a blazer at the front side) extends an arm (opYQsnkV1Vs 1:49.5).
- **Bench, clearest sequence** (NLGyEXp8nJM 3:25-3:39; eu_9nPA3zRs 101:46-101:49.5): the head referee stands at the HEAD END of the bench beside the hand-off spotter. (1) Holds the RIGHT ARM straight out horizontally over the lifter, palm flat and down, while the lifter settles locked out (~2 s typical, 9 s seen). (2) Sweeps it sharply DOWN = "start"; the bar starts down at once. (3) Arm low and forward while the bar is on the chest; he leans forward over the bench at the pause (94:52). (4) Lifts or flicks it = "press". (5) Points forward after lockout = "rack". Sweeps are crisp, ~0.3-0.5 s.
- **Deadlift.** The centre referee holds an arm out level, palm down, during the pull, ready to give "down" (c5oY-m6ypNk 8:34-8:39); "down" comes ~1.5-3 s after lockout. A centre referee gave the jury a thumbs-up after a 2-1 (dHn-gvJpzPU 1:15).
- Side referees sit on chairs at the sides of the platform (Avancus: in blazers, at small tables at the back corners).

### 6.5 Spotters during the lift

- Squat: all 4-5 crouch with the lifter, hands cupped under the bar ends, and rise with him; on the rack they guide the ends into the hooks.
- Bench: the two side spotters keep both hands cupped a few cm under the sleeves for the WHOLE lift, following the bar down and up without touching (eu_9nPA3zRs 91:10-91:13, 124:43.5-124:50.5).
- Deadlift: none during the pull; loaders rush in the instant the bar lands (zsVGiAJOnNA 4:29-4:31).


## 7. Lights and decisions

### 7.1 When they appear

- Squat/bench: 1-5 s after the rack (Avancus 3-4 s, eu_9nPA3zRs 22:12; bench ~1 s after the lifter sits up, 95:44). Deadlift: 1.5-2.5 s after the bar lands (A5).
- IPF stream: a single dot appears the moment the bar is down, the three circles ~0.5 s later, ~2.5-3 s after lockout (oGfa7HoDrrA 0:40.5-0:41). Worlds squat lights came within ~1 s of standing (lNOrz4CVNMY 0:41.5-0:42).
- Measured gaps in the Sheffield 2025 edits were longer on deadlifts: 3.5-6 s after the bar was down (c6JJZD1W-QA 8:29.5-8:34; Mw95YfGN7Vg 8:36-8:42), ~10 s once on a bench (LxSRpjl_UGU 4:14-4:24), although that batch's summary says "1-2 s".
- A contested call can take ~18 s (A3-14, 102:43-103:03); the lifter had already walked off and was talking to her coach while the next lifter stood waiting backstage ~11 s.
- Very often the lights arrive while the lifter is already walking off, or even after he is backstage (FwruiLgnapQ 4:22; eu_9nPA3zRs 130:12-130:25, a no-lift learned backstage by looking up at a monitor ~10 s with the coach).

### 7.2 Who looks, and for how long

- Most lifters do NOT stand and stare. They know, they turn away, and they glance back over the shoulder while walking (22:13-22:14.5, 24:15, 26:10).
- Those who wait stand perfectly still, belt on, arms hanging, head turned to the lights: 1.5 s (142:28.5-142:29.75), 3-4 s (175:03-175:04; 210:05-210:08.5), up to ~8 s (201:54-202:02). They celebrate only when the call comes. Only 2 of 9 lifters in the men's final deadlift round waited ([[Meet notes A6]]).
- Depth-call victims turn the head toward the side judge (60:03-60:05, 69:50-69:52, 77:00-77:03).
- On a 2-1 good lift with a red, a lifter may stay stone-faced and look up at the scoreboard, head tilted back, because the jury could still overturn it (opYQsnkV1Vs 3:09.5-3:11).

> **Disagreement.** [[Meet notes S2]] says lifters "often do not look at the lights", while [[Meet notes S1]] lists "a look back over the shoulder at the lights or scoreboard" as very common in miss reactions. Both are true: nobody stands and watches the lights on a clear lift; a quick look back while walking is common when the lift was close or missed.

### 7.3 Jury overturns and cards

- Overturns seen: a woman's squat opener passed 2-1 and later overturned (HviCculb2Vw, graphic at 1:05 shows -190 in red); a squat passed on the platform and overturned after review (NLGyEXp8nJM 2:54); a 2-1 bench with a yellow card overturned to no lift; the lifter walked straight to the officials' table, leaned over to a seated official, pointed at her wrist wrap, then walked off (9VK3zm60ohg 5:00-5:12).
- Avancus: the commentary says the jury cannot intervene there (eu_9nPA3zRs 188:32).
- Card colours under a red X: red, blue, yellow, each a reason code; the broadcast printed the reason ("Elbow depth" 95:44; "Lifting position changed during lift" 103:01; "Downward movement" 114:29). Commentators disputed "blue" cards (HviCculb2Vw 3:43, 4:42).

## 8. Celebrations as rules

The full move-by-move list of every celebration is in [[Meet Celebration Catalogue]]. This section gives the rules an animator or designer needs.

### 8.1 How often lifters celebrate at all

| Batch | What was counted | Result |
|---|---|---|
| [[Meet notes A1]] women's squats | 20 attempts | ~10 had a visible gesture, all small to medium; openers almost nothing |
| [[Meet notes A3]] bench | 5 men's openers; 27 women's benches | men 0 of 5; women 7 real celebrations, mostly on 3rd attempts and PBs |
| [[Meet notes A4]] bench + deadlift | 25 men's benches; 8 women's deadlift openers | men 8; women 1 real + 3 small |
| [[Meet notes A5]] deadlift | 39 attempts | ~21 with some gesture, ~10 with none; men's rounds 1-2 mostly nothing |
| [[Meet notes A6]] men's last deadlifts | 9 attempts | arms overhead on the platform 2; kneel 2; hug at the exit 9 of 9 |
| [[Meet notes C2]] Sheffield + Worlds battles | 45 attempts | ~18 with a visible reaction (E5 x3, E4 x2, E3 x4, E2 x6, E1 x3) |

Highlight compilations (S1, C3, X1 and the Sheffield "best of") over-represent big celebrations; the full-meet broadcast ([[Meet notes A1]]-[[Meet notes A6]]) is the honest base rate: **about half of all good attempts get some gesture, and only a few per session get a big one.**

### 8.2 Rule 1: size scales with the stakes, then personality overrides it

- Openers get nothing or a nod ("no fireworks on an opener", commentary, [[Meet notes A3]]; eu_9nPA3zRs 19:55-19:57; none on a 22.5 kg jump, lNOrz4CVNMY 4:46-4:50). Energy grows through the meet ([[Meet notes C2]]).
- The big ones come on 3rd attempts, PRs, records, upsets, winning lifts, a rescue after a missed attempt, and especially on hard-fought GRINDERS: the loudest celebrations of the whole broadcast came from 93 kg men hitting PR third squats on grinders (eu_9nPA3zRs 70:50-70:57, 75:52-75:58), not from the heaviest lifts.
- Personality beats stakes. The biggest weights often get the smallest reactions: 430 kg squat, no reaction at all (57:45-57:51); 485 kg squat world record, a grin and a long hug, no arms raised (78:37.8-78:46); the all-time tested total record, one finger point while the LED wall celebrates (210:02-210:18); a 275 kg PB bench, nothing (134:04-134:12); an open world-record deadlift, walks off with no gesture (c5oY-m6ypNk 7:08-7:11); a Sheffield woman who stays at E1-2 even for world records (8lBTmPY5_Jc). A showman gave his world-record squat only a small wave but his third squat a 7-s roar (Mw95YfGN7Vg 1:56 vs 2:53-3:00).

### 8.3 Rule 2: timing (before or after the lights)

- Clear lift: the reaction starts the moment the bar is racked or down, or as the lights come; the lights land in the middle of it (eu_9nPA3zRs 70:50-70:51, 203:01-203:08; c6JJZD1W-QA 9:44.5-9:46).
- Close lift: the lifter stands perfectly still, staring at the head referee or the board for 1.5-4 s, and only explodes when the call comes (eu_9nPA3zRs 142:28-142:31.5, 175:03-175:04.5, 176:03-176:04; FwruiLgnapQ 6:39-6:41).
- 2-1 with a red: may stay stone-faced, because the jury can still overturn it (opYQsnkV1Vs 3:09.5-3:11).
- It goes wrong sometimes: one lifter clapped and spread his arms, then got red lights (c6JJZD1W-QA 8:30-8:34); another raised his fist and the reds appeared under his arm (LxSRpjl_UGU 8:05-8:07).

> **Disagreement.** The batches disagree on how often lifters wait. [[Meet notes S1]] says "nobody celebrated before the lights" and [[Meet notes X1]] says every big one came after the lights; [[Meet notes C3]] says big celebrations start BEFORE the lights on most big lifts (8+ cases); [[Meet notes C4]] counts ~11 waited vs ~6 did not; [[Meet notes A4]] says mostly before the lights while walking off. Even S1's own raw notes have one arm fling a second before the lights graphic (NLGyEXp8nJM 6:58.5 vs 6:59.5). Likely reason: broadcast graphics lag the real light box, and lifters often read the floor lights or simply know. Safe rule: start on clear lifts, wait on close ones.

### 8.4 Rule 3: where it happens

- **Not planted at the bar.** Nobody stays on the platform long. On the platform there is usually one short beat (step back, one arm up, a roar in a half crouch, a clap) and the rest is done WHILE WALKING OFF (eu_9nPA3zRs 31:10-31:13, 203:04-203:13; [[Meet notes C4]] "done while walking off, mostly toward the crowd at the stage front edge").
- **Avancus:** the big roar or pose happens AFTER the lifter walks off the back of the platform, in front of the backstage "GOOD LIFT" light wall where a steadicam waits (eu_9nPA3zRs 70:54, 72:38, 73:42, 74:52, 75:55, 107:42-107:44).
- **Sheffield:** showmen walk TOWARD the front camera and the crowd at the stage edge for 3-6 s, then hug at the exit ([[Meet notes S1]], c6JJZD1W-QA, Mw95YfGN7Vg 2:53-3:00).
- **On the floor of the platform:** the biggest emotional moments use the floor (section 8.6).
- **At the exit with the coach or partner:** the hug is the real end of almost every attempt (8.5).

> **Disagreement.** [[Meet notes S1]] places men's big celebrations on the platform walking toward the camera; [[Meet notes S2]] says the biggest happen OFF the platform at the exit (though its own three biggest all begin on the platform). Both describe one chain: starts on the platform, peaks walking off, ends in the hug.

### 8.5 Rule 4: the hug always comes last

- A coach or team greeting at the exit ends almost every attempt, good or missed: ~25 of 39 attempts ([[Meet notes A5]]); every one of 9 ([[Meet notes A6]]). Types and how often are in section 10.
- On the biggest moments: a coach rushes in for a bear hug (eu_9nPA3zRs 74:52.5, 78:41); a ~10 s rocking bear hug (opYQsnkV1Vs 7:17-7:26; FwruiLgnapQ 9:24-9:31); a partner or coach jumps into the lifter's arms, legs round his waist (opYQsnkV1Vs 0:48.3; ay2OvIWZUcU 2:12.0) or the lifter jumps into the coach's arms (3i4jvmDrtdY 6:03.5); women lifted off their feet by coaches several times (9FQM0Z6_s8Q 2:57-2:58.5; gfp-IdEaQNc 11:27-11:30).

### 8.7 Men vs women, light vs heavy

- **Men:** roars (crouched, at the ceiling, into the camera from ~1 m), flexes (double biceps, "crab" squeeze with fists in front of the hips), fists hammered down toward the knees, stomps and jumps, floor moves, arms-spread poses. Energy is often in the legs and fists more than overhead arms ([[Meet notes X1]]).
- **Women:** arms up in a V, overhead claps, fist pumps in front of the chest, hair flips, hands on the head or over the face, skips, heart hands, finger guns, high-fives with every spotter; sitting or lying down and crying after huge lifts; being lifted by the coach. Women's celebrations at Avancus were as big as the men's openers ([[Meet notes A2]]), and the top end is just as big (first-ever women's 300 kg squat: drops to her knees sobbing, 3i4jvmDrtdY 1:04-1:13.5).
- **Light lifters** (47-74 kg) are bouncy: skipping walks, shimmies, stomps, deep crouches (NLGyEXp8nJM 0:53-0:57; UnUJPzEesc4 5:17).
- **Super-heavies** are mostly calm or stoic (430 kg nothing; 485 kg grin; LxSRpjl_UGU E0 on most lifts; short shuffling walk), with occasional huge exceptions (a sky-point with both index fingers, eu_9nPA3zRs 143:30.5; a tuck jump after an upset, opYQsnkV1Vs 7:10.3).

### 8.8 Signature celebrations

Like rituals, celebrations repeat per lifter: one man did the same "both fists up beside the head, one pump at the chest" on both deadlifts (eu_9nPA3zRs 186:34, 195:44); one woman did "one clap, throw the hair back" on both (161:17, 169:25); one man did "wings" (arms straight out sideways) three times (lNOrz4CVNMY 17:32, 20:55, 24:38).

### 9.4 Reactions to a miss, and how often

- **Most misses are calm** (every batch says so). Ranked by frequency:
  1. Blank or neutral face, walk off, maybe one look back over the shoulder at the lights or board (eu_9nPA3zRs 22:13-22:14.5, 58:40-58:46; qzFjTLcwmHY 1:30.5; most broadcast misses).
  2. Rueful smile, small shrug, open-palm "oh well", laughing it off (eu_9nPA3zRs 47:03.5, 114:28-114:31, 139:42; up-LEMKGRA8 9:56.5, 16:38.5-16:41.5).
  3. A short stare: up at the screen ~2-5 s, or at the side judge (LxSRpjl_UGU 2:40.5-2:42; Mw95YfGN7Vg 8:42.5-8:44; eu_9nPA3zRs 60:03-60:05, 183:03-183:09 a stunned 6 s stare).
  4. Hand on head / both hands on top of the head / hand over the face (FwruiLgnapQ 5:43.5; lNOrz4CVNMY 23:25-23:25.5; up-LEMKGRA8 11:24; FkQMMNSD6HI 5:55).
  5. Thanks to the crowd anyway: arms up, an open-hand wave, prayer hands (eu_9nPA3zRs 171:57, 211:12.5; qzFjTLcwmHY 4:47.5-4:49, 7:21.5).
  6. Asking the judge: walks to the seated judge and asks, palms up, nodding; or leans over the jury table (FkQMMNSD6HI 4:16.5-4:20, 5:56-5:57.5; c6JJZD1W-QA 5:50-5:56; 9VK3zm60ohg 5:00-5:12). A nod of agreement to the referees (YLMRf70ALKo 7:30-7:34).
  7. Head tipped back to the ceiling, mouth open (eu_9nPA3zRs 68:47-68:49); kneeling and looking up at the lights ~4 s (209:18-209:23.5).
- **Big reactions are rare and come at elimination or near-misses:** face buried in both hands over the racked bar ~3 s after a bomb-out (A5A39IMP3gs 1:19-1:22); a scream, half laugh half cry, then both open hands up "that's all I had" (oGfa7HoDrrA 7:41-7:43); flinging an arm, jumping, falling to the knees after a grip miss for a world title (jfMBhKh4ab4 1:36-1:44); the anger belt rip (lNOrz4CVNMY 19:57.5); a stunned open-mouthed laugh at two unexplained reds (HviCculb2Vw 4:43-4:44); an annoyed hop with one knee pulled up (zsVGiAJOnNA 5:33).
- Nobody in any meet video threw gear at anything, kicked the bar or shouted at officials ([[Meet notes S1]], [[Meet notes S2]], [[Meet notes A2]]). Ripping off wrist wraps angrily was seen only in a gym clip (iIvTXa3Hdlo 3:52-3:54).
- The crowd can cheer a miss: standing and cheering a failed world-record try anyway (oGfa7HoDrrA 11:50-11:52); quiet with hands over mouths after a faint (A5A39IMP3gs 4:17-4:27).
- The coach consoles: arm round the shoulders (HviCculb2Vw 8:35-8:36; oGfa7HoDrrA 4:29.5-4:31.5), a palm on the cheek backstage (A5A39IMP3gs 1:30-1:32), a hand on the shoulder (eu_9nPA3zRs 136:11.5).
- No faints were seen in the full meet broadcast; the three faints and collapses are from IPF compilations (A5A39IMP3gs 4:00; UnUJPzEesc4 6:56, 9:27). The meet simply carries on afterwards (UnUJPzEesc4 9:38-9:42).


## 10. Going back

### 10.1 The walk-off

- **Route.** Avancus: off the back of the platform / the stage-left wing down into the backstage area behind the logo wall, the same way the lifter came in (eu_9nPA3zRs 19:55-19:57, 49:36-49:41); in the last session the exit is described as stage right past the front row and side tables to the tunnel (A6). Sheffield: off the front or side past the seated side referee and the floor light box, or out the back corner, often through a GREEN-washed exit area (opYQsnkV1Vs 0:52-0:55; c6JJZD1W-QA). IPF Worlds: along the stage past the referee table to the backdrop exit, behind an ESN banner (oGfa7HoDrrA 0:41; YLMRf70ALKo 11:00).
- **Pace.** Normal walking pace, arms loose; one man jogged off as his whole celebration (eu_9nPA3zRs 194:51); one ran straight into the crowd (dHn-gvJpzPU 3:40.5-3:47).
- **Squat:** ducks out from under the racked bar to one side, turns, walks off; often looks back over the shoulder once. **Bench:** lie 1-2 s, sit up (crunch or leg-swing rock-up), sit ~1 s hands on knees, stand, shake the arms out once, walk off pulling the LEFT wrist-wrap velcro open with the RIGHT hand (eu_9nPA3zRs 91:19, 118:19.5, 121:45, 131:15). **Deadlift:** stays bent over the bar ~1 s, stands, walks off, often unbuckling the belt.
- A handheld or steadicam operator follows or walks backwards in front of the lifter.
- The loaders are already walking in as the lifter leaves (oGfa7HoDrrA 0:42), and swarm the bar while a bench lifter is still sitting (eu_9nPA3zRs 91:17).
- The lower third already shows the NEXT lifter's name while the camera follows this one off (c6JJZD1W-QA 0:52-0:54).
- Right after the lift the next attempt is handed in on an attempt card to clipboard staff (dHn-gvJpzPU 2:11-2:12).

## 12. Variety

### 12.1 Men vs women

| | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|

### 12.2 Classes and body types

- **Light (47-66 kg):** fast and bouncy; very wide squat grips on light men (1.7-2x shoulder width, YLMRf70ALKo; up-LEMKGRA8 2:16); a short lifter adjusts foot blocks under his feet on the bench (c6JJZD1W-QA 4:51-4:56); skipping walk-offs.
- **Middle (74-105 kg):** the loudest celebrations; low-bar 74 kg men grip just outside the shoulders with elbows up (UnUJPzEesc4 4:48); squat tempo varies by person (66 kg man slow 2 s down / 3-3.5 s up every time; 83 kg man ~1 s down, a 0.3 s bounce, ~1.2 s up; c6JJZD1W-QA, Mw95YfGN7Vg).
- **Super-heavy (120+):** grip almost at the collars (~1.7x); very wide stance, toes out 30-40 deg; 3-4 short shuffling walkout steps "very unsteady"; slow heavy walk; head down; the bar visibly bends (eu_9nPA3zRs 67:07-67:10); 8 plates a side; little celebrating ([[Meet notes C4]] lifter E0 on most lifts); 5 spotters or more.
- **Equipped lifters** (old Worlds, US bench meet): suits with straps up, knee wraps; a bench shirt holds the arms straight out in front even when standing (FnFKPop-3IE 0:00-0:15).
- **Juniors and sub-juniors:** a 16-year-old at Age Nationals; juniors at Worlds; the same rituals, smaller crowds.

### 12.3 Signature rituals

See 4.4. Every batch that followed one lifter across several attempts found one or two fixed rituals per lifter, repeated every attempt (wall lean, headband blindfold, palms on ears, arms up on entry, T-pose strut, shoulder slaps, fist bumps with every loader, ear hold by the handler).

### 12.4 What computer lifters need so they read as real

1. **One personality type each**, chosen from what was seen: quiet/stoic (walks off, E1), calm-proud (one arm up, a nod), joyful showman (waves, bows, arms wide, palms up), fiery (claps, roars, belt rip, hops), emotional (hands over face, kneeling, tears), crowd-worker (ear cup, "louder" flaps). The study found all six at every big meet ([[Meet notes C4]]: "quiet-then-explosive, showman, fiery, stoic").
2. **One or two signature rituals** repeated every attempt (section 4.4), plus one signature celebration.
3. **Build-driven body language:** grip width, walkout steps, walk speed, squat tempo and celebration energy scale with the build (light = fast and bouncy; super-heavy = slow, shuffling, calm).
4. **A coach or handler** who does something before (traps, ears, ammonia, talk) and greets them after.
5. **Variation in setup length** (deadlift sumo 15+ s vs conventional ~3 s; squat wedge 3-25 s).
6. **Realistic hit rate:** roughly 1 attempt in 5 fails overall, but it varies a lot by session: 10 of 38 in the men's squats ([[Meet notes A2]]), 1 of 20 in the women's squats ([[Meet notes A1]]), 3 of 39 in a deadlift block ([[Meet notes A5]]), 13 of 60 in the Sheffield/Worlds edits ([[Meet notes S2]]); misses come mostly on 3rd attempts and record tries.
7. **Women's hair and gear routines**, and gear variety (headbands, chains, coloured wraps, knee-high socks).


## 13. What a game would get wrong, and design implications

### 13.1 What a game would most likely get wrong (concrete)

1. **Rushing the setup.** Real lifters stand bent under the squat bar with the head bowed 8-15 s (up to 25 s), stand braced 4-11 s before "squat", lie 5-18 s setting up a bench, and wedge 5-20 s in a deadlift with hips high until the last ~0.5 s (eu_9nPA3zRs 69:20-69:35, 92:56-93:13; oGfa7HoDrrA 0:33.5-0:36.75). An unrack-and-squat in 2 s looks fake.
2. **Static spotters.** All squat spotters crouch with every rep, hands cupped under the ends; bench side spotters follow the sleeves the whole lift; three lift a missed bench together. Spotters who only move on a miss look wrong.
3. **Celebrating every good lift.** Openers get nothing; about half of all good lifts get any gesture; big ones are a few per session and scale with stakes and personality. The biggest weights often get the smallest reactions.
4. **Planting the lifter at the bar for a celebration.** Real celebrations happen while walking off, toward the crowd or backstage, and end in a hug.
5. **Everyone staring at the lights.** Most lifters turn away and glance back; the venue (LED wall, colour wash, light box) tells the audience.
6. **One miss animation, with drama.** There are many miss bodies (stall, dump, faint, fall forward, fall backward, grip failure, lockout stall); reactions are mostly calm; nobody throws anything.
7. **Dropping the deadlift.** Good deadlifts are lowered with both hands after "down"; dropping from the top is a real no-lift and never happened on a good lift.
8. **Wrong approach.** Sheffield-style rack approach is from BEHIND the uprights so the lifter faces the crowd; the walkout is 2-3 short steps, not a long back-pedal.
9. **Hype in the wrong place.** Pre-lift hype is done BESIDE the rack or behind the bar before touching it, or on the walk-on; coach slaps, ammonia and ear holds happen backstage, not on the platform.
10. **Random rituals.** Each lifter has one ritual repeated every attempt.
11. **A bright gym look for every meet.** Big shows are dark theatres with one top light, haze and LED walls; IPF Worlds is bright and plain; Nationals is a warehouse hall; hotel ballrooms exist.
12. **No commands.** The bench "start" (flat arm held out, then dropped), the "press" pause on the chest, "rack", and the deadlift "down" are visible beats with waits around them.

### 13.2 Design implications for this game (recommendations for later, nothing built)

These are suggestions to discuss before any implementation. They are written against the existing systems (turn-based meets with sit-and-wait, State-level caller, coach cutscene, celebration catalogue, unlockable emotes, NPC gear and body types) and must respect GAME_DESIGN.md where it already decides something.

1. **Attempt timeline as data.** Drive every attempt from a per-lift timeline with measured ranges (section 2) rather than fixed animation lengths: e.g. squat wedge 4-15 s, braced wait 4-10 s, bench lying setup 5-15 s, deadlift wedge 3-15 s (sumo longer). Let the player skip the waits for themselves, but keep them for NPCs and spectators.
2. **Celebration chooser by rule, not random.** Pick celebration size from attempt number, PR/record/win flags, grinder vs easy, and the lifter's personality; clear lifts react on rack/down, close lifts wait and stare 1.5-4 s; occasionally let an NPC celebrate and then get reds. Keep the full move list in [[Meet Celebration Catalogue]].
3. **Celebrate on the move.** Author celebrations as "beat at the bar (0.5-1.5 s) -> walk-off moves -> exit hug" so they never freeze the lifter on the platform; the exit hug or clasp is the default ending for every attempt.
4. **Spotter and loader crew as a system.** 3-5 crew per lift type with crouch-with-the-lifter squat poses, sleeve-following bench hands, the three-person bench save, miss catches per miss type, plate changes with a bar jack between lifts, clapping after big lifts. Performance: reuse one crew mesh and few animations.
5. **Miss library.** At least: squat stall + assisted stand-up, squat bail, depth no-lift (normal rep), bench sink + three-spotter save, bench technical red, deadlift no-break, stall below/at the knee, lockout stall, grip failure (arms fly up), down-too-early. Faints and falls are real but should be very rare or left out (they may read as injury in a kids' game).
6. **Referee gestures and command beats.** Animate the head referee's bench arm (out, sweep down = start), the press pause, rack/down points, and the side referees in chairs. Commands can be voiced (the project already has TTS) with the visible gesture.
7. **Lights shown by the venue.** Three-dot graphic plus a venue reaction that scales with meet level: Local = a light box only; State = light box + stage colour wash; Nationals = LED "GOOD LIFT" / "NO LIFT"; Worlds/Sheffield-style = name-card intros, green/red washes, "NEW RECORD" animations, purple light for record attempts, maybe spark fountains. Cheap and reads as "big meet" instantly (several batches said so).
8. **Per-lifter signature ritual + personality.** Give every NPC one ritual from section 4.4 and one personality from 12.4; pair this with the planned unlockable emotes so player rituals can be chosen and repeated.
9. **Coach and backstage moments.** Before attempts: trap kneading, ear hold, ammonia, headphones off, chalk; after: hug types from 10.2. Keep ammonia and slaps optional/cosmetic.
10. **Belt as a prop.** Lever belt worn low; tighten at the bar; unbuckle while walking off after deadlifts; rare celebration rip-and-swing; the gym-style one-hand take-off flick for training. Sound: a sharp lever snap.
11. **Meet presentation by level.** Use the venue table (11.4) as a reference: dark theatre with LED walls for invitationals, bright IPF hall for Worlds, warehouse hall for Nationals, ballroom option; awards with a line-up in identical kit, places 4+ medalled in the line, podium 3-2-1, the winner hugging rivals.
12. **Crowd.** Loud during the lift, name chants, stand for records, flags, phones up; quiet hands-over-mouths after a scary miss. Matches the "real meet atmosphere" rule already in the project.
13. **Performance warning.** Haze, LED walls, spark fountains, many animated crew and crowd members all cost performance; reuse meshes, keep crowd animation cheap (few loops, distance-based), and run the performance audit after building any of this.
