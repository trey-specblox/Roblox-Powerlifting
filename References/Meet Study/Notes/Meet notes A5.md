## 4. Before the platform, entrances, walk-ups, rituals
- ON-DECK TUNNEL (every attempt): lifter stands in front of an AVANCUS-logo backdrop with a coach, belt already on,
  usually still and staring ahead 5-15 s (man L 189:15-189:27; man Q 193:36-193:47). Coach talks face to face.
- Coach actions seen: kneading the TRAPS/shoulders from behind ~14 s (172:03-172:17); cinching the BELT from behind
  (166:01, 184:36-184:38.5) or from the front (188:20-188:22); AMMONIA capsule held under the nose (162:31.5-162:34,
  171:15-171:16, 188:27) -> head snaps back, mouth opens, one arm flung up (171:16.0); POWDERING the lifter's THIGHS for a
  sumo pull (162:34.5-162:36); clipboards/attempt cards; a green towel; handing over / taking headphones.
- Lifter self-prep: over-ear HEADPHONES on until called (160:31, 179:34, 185:13-185:21, 196:02), then lifted off over the
  head with both hands; earbuds taken out (188:16); CHALK at a chalk bowl on a stand in the tunnel, hands rubbed and
  clapped (168:50, 179:44, 197:04, 197:53); pacing small circles / facing the wall during a delay (197:24-197:29); hands on
  head; high-fives/daps on the way out (185:29.5-185:30.5).
- ENTRANCE: walk is normal pace, no strut, arms loose, often hands on hips (183:42, 184:42, 192:50). A steadicam operator
  walks right beside/behind the lifter on the platform. Gestures seen on entrance: right arm straight up/open hand
  (162:40.5), arm up + point + fist (171:19-171:21), arm to the roof + point at crowd (195:19-195:20).
- PRE-PULL RITUALS at the bar: belt yanked tighter with both hands, elbows out (very common, ~12x); EAR-CUP to the crowd
  (163:00.3-163:01.3); ARM SWINGS loosening (171:40-171:41.5, 175:38); hands to the back of the head fixing the ponytail
  (171:42, 175:35); "T" ARM SPREAD stretch, palms forward, chest out ~1 s (185:39.5, 187:08, 193:58 — 2 men, 3x);
  shaking out dangling hands at the bar (195:29-195:37); bending to the bar then standing back up to re-set (~6x).
- The LED wall shows each lifter's name in giant letters + flag + a posed photo (flexing, heart hands) during the walk-on.

## 5. Going up to the bar, holding the bar, setup
- Stance: majority SUMO (wide, toes out, hands inside the knees) for women and most 93 kg men; conventional for the
  heaviest men (SHW, man N, man Q) and two women.
- Feet: low close-up shots show 2-6 small shuffles to set the feet (170:16-170:19, 193:00-193:05); hip-width for
  conventional.
- Grip: one hand down then the other (167:18 right hand first); hands just outside the shins (conventional) or inside the
  knees (sumo). Grip type not readable at this resolution.
- SETUP DURATION is long: women commonly wedged/crouched 5-11 s before the pull (165:23-165:33, 169:08-169:19, 170:34-
  170:44); men 3-8 s. Many lifters crouch, stand back up, re-crouch.
- Sumo wedge look: hips low, chest tall, arms long and straight, eyes forward/slightly up, still.

## 6. The lift itself
- EASY pulls: floor to lockout 0.5-1.5 s (men's openers 0.5-1 s: 185:46.5->185:47.0, 187:16.5->187:17.0, 188:02.5->188:03.0).
- GRINDERS: 2.5-5.5 s. Typical shape: fast off the floor, slows just above the knee, hips creep forward, head tips back
  before the shoulders come back, face grimaces, lips pressed or mouth opens (166:36-166:39; 170:44.5-170:47; 172:50.5-
  172:56 = 5.5 s; 175:55.5-175:59).
- Lockout: stand tall, shoulders back, often head tilted back; hold 1-4 s (waiting for the head ref's "down"); lifters look
  at the head referee during the hold (168:28-168:29).
- Lowering: always controlled with both hands on the bar, hips back; never dropped from the top in a good lift. Several
  stay bent over the bar for ~1 s after it lands (161:16.8, 199:14.5-199:15).
- Faces: intense brows-down before the pull (163:53), open-mouth yell or grimace at lockout on PB/3rd attempts (174:02).

## 7. Completing, the lights, misses and reactions to a miss
- Lights are not shown as physical lights in shot; the broadcast graphic lower-left shows three circles that become
  three ticks (good) or three red marks (no lift) ~1.5-2.5 s after the bar lands; the LED wall then shows "GOOD LIFT" or
  "NO LIFT" in giant animated text over the empty platform.
- Lifters on uncertain lifts stand still behind the bar staring at the head ref (170:52-170:56; 175:03-175:04;
  176:03-176:03.5) and only celebrate when the call comes.
- MISS types seen: (1) bar stalls at mid-shin below the knee, lifter lets go and it drops back (171:52-171:52.8); (2) bar
  lowered before the "down" command / lost balance after a fast lockout (182:57.5); (3) hitch near lockout, lowered without
  "down" (199:13-199:13.8).
- Miss reactions: hands to face then a smile and an arms-up salute (woman D 171:53-171:57); a 6 s blank, stunned stare at
  the platform edge (man L 183:03-183:09); flat frown, belt unbuckled, eyes down (man S 199:16.5-199:19). Nobody threw
  anything or shouted in anger.

## 10. What surprised me / what a game would most likely get wrong
1. The setup is LONG: 5-11 s crouched in the wedge before the pull (165:23-165:33, 170:34-170:44). A game that pulls 1 s
   after gripping will look fake.
2. The pull itself is SHORT on easy lifts (0.5-1 s) and the hold at the top is 1-4 s. Real pacing: long setup, fast pull,
   long still hold, controlled lower.
3. Men in deadlift rounds 1-2 mostly do NOTHING after the lift: stand, unbuckle, walk off. Celebration energy belongs to
   3rd attempts/PBs/wins. Don't make every opener a roar.
4. Celebrations often WAIT for the call on close lifts (175:03-175:04.5, 176:03-176:04): stand still, stare at the head
   ref, then explode.
5. The coach hug backstage is near-universal and is where much of the emotion happens (not on stage).
6. Grinders: the head goes back before the shoulders; the slowdown is just above the knee; 3-5.5 s total (172:50.5-172:56).
7. Misses are calm: lets go and stands, hands to face, blank stare, or even a salute. No tantrums.
8. Pre-lift rituals are small and specific: belt yanks, ear-cup, T-arm stretch, arm swings, ponytail tightening,
   ammonia on deck, thigh powder for sumo — not big chest-slapping hype at the bar in this range.
9. The bar is lowered WITH the hands, not dropped; lifters often stay bent over it a moment after it lands.
10. Presentation details a game can copy cheaply: LED wall name card, "GOOD LIFT"/"NO LIFT" wall text, lower-left 3-light
    ticks, "X -> Y IF SUCCESSFUL" graphic, standings during loading, red chevron wipe into a replay.
