### Platform, crew and presentation (deadlift session 200-211)
- Platform: raised dark stage, a light-grey lifting platform edged with a glowing white LED strip; dark arena, the

- Behind the platform: a GIANT LED wall (full stage width) — shows the lifter's intro (name + flag + portrait video),
  the meet logo "HOUSTON" between lifts, "GOOD LIFT"/"NO LIFT" after, and "AMERICAN RECORD ATTEMPT" / "NEW AMERICAN
  RECORD" for records. It flashes the brand red on a big good lift (203:08).
- Lighting states: normal white top light; PURPLE/blue wash for record attempts; RED wash after a miss (211:12-16).
- Judges: seated at small tables on each side at the back corners (side referees) — visible as dark figures on
  both sides of the wide shot with the plate racks/benches beside them.
- Loaders: 4-5 in black "HOUSTON" T-shirts. Between lifters they jog on, two at each end, slide the collars off,
  swap plates (red 25 kg, yellow 15 kg / smaller change plates), re-collar and jog off in ~10-15 s (203:28-203:57,
  208:09-208:14). After the final attempt they strip the bar completely (211:22-211:32).
- Spotters in deadlift: none stand behind the lifter; 2-3 crew stand at the back corner tables.
- Broadcast graphics: lower-third with name, flag, attempt weights (green = good, red = miss); a big "STANDINGS"
  table (10 rows) shown full-screen between lifters for 15-60 s; a top-left banner for records; a right-side card

  during long breaks.

## 10. Surprises / what a game would get wrong
1. The biggest lift of the day got the SMALLEST reaction: the all-time total record (210:02-210:18) was one finger
   point and a hug; the LED wall, lighting and commentators did the celebrating. A game that makes every record
   lifter roar would feel fake; let the ARENA celebrate and give the lifter a calm/proud option.
2. Men's celebrations here are joyful and open (palms up, arms in a V, waves, bows, prayer hands, a kneel) more than
   angry roaring. Only one entrance roar (205:40) and one shout at the top (207:04.5). Mix the two families.
3. Nobody stays on the platform long: after "down" the celebration happens WHILE WALKING OFF toward stage right
   (203:04-203:13). A game that plants the lifter in one spot for a 10 s animation will look wrong.
4. The belt comes off on the walk-off for most lifters, good or miss; once ripped off and swung overhead
   (203:05.5). The belt is a celebration prop.
5. Every lifter, every outcome, ends in a hug at the exit (coach or team) — the hug is the real end of every
   attempt.
6. Lifters lower the deadlift WITH the bar after "down" (squat down with it), they don't drop it from the top.
7. Missed deadlifts are short and undramatic: bar to mid-shin, stops, put down in ~2 s, then a wave or a kneel. No
   anger, no gear thrown.
8. The lights are rarely watched for long: only 2 of 9 lifters stood and waited (4-8 s, perfectly still). Most turn
   away immediately because they know.
9. Presentation does a lot: LED intro wall with the lifter's name, flag and video portrait at every walk-on; purple
   light for record attempts; red after a miss; "GOOD LIFT" lettering. Cheap to do in a game and hugely effective.
10. Awards: one long line of ~20 athletes all in identical white kit, only the top 3 go on the podium, trophies are
    gold shoes + flowers, medals put over the head in the line; the winner hugs her rivals before stepping up
    (223:44). A game should not use singlets or belts at the ceremony.
