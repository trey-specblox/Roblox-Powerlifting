## 10. What surprised me / what a game would get wrong
1. The 20:53 world-record celebration is short and physical: roar DURING the lockout hold, fast drop, V-arms jump, wide arms, then thumbs up and pointing at himself backstage. The crowd shot (20:53-20:54.8) is half the moment: a standing block, arms up clapping overhead, palms on heads, phones up.
2. IPF men almost never celebrate a normal good lift: 22.5 kg jumps, PRs and lead-taking lifts get a fist bump or low hand slap at most (4:46, 7:59, 14:50). Big celebrations are for records, grinders and winners. A game that celebrates every good lift will look fake.
3. Each lifter has a SIGNATURE that repeats every attempt (GBR B's hang-and-kneel under the squat bar; HUN's arms-out "wings"). Give each lifter one personal ritual and reuse it.
4. Long waits: 4-10 s standing with the squat bar before the command, 8-25 s deadlift setups that include standing back up and re-bending. Games usually rush this.
5. Spotters crouch WITH the squatter all the way down and up, palms cupped under the bar ends (0:34.5-0:37.5), and walk the bar back into the hooks with the lifter.
6. Grinders are far longer than games show: 7-8 s squat, 8 s and 12 s bench presses with the bar almost not moving.
7. Misses have distinct readable shapes (stuck in the hole + spotters lift; bench stall + three spotters; deadlift barely breaking the floor or stalling at the knee) and distinct reactions (calm, guarding an injury, belt rip-off, hands on head).
8. Deadlifts are lowered under control with the hands on the bar; the only fast drop seen was the record celebration.
9. IPF coaches meet lifters just off stage, never on the platform; Sheffield shows big hugs and coaches holding heads and necks backstage before lifts.
10. The featured woman stays understated even with world records (small smile, low high-five); the loudest women's moments were a collapse to the knees crying (first 300 kg squat) and sitting on the floor laughing then jumping into the coach's arms.
