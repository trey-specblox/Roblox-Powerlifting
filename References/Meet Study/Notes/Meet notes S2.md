## 5. Going up to the bar, holding the bar, setup

- SQUAT: walks to the rack from behind and stands centred between the uprights for several seconds. Both hands go up to the bar together at Sheffield (V1 0:45) but in V7 0:02-0:09 the lifter stares at the bar ~4 s, then reaches the RIGHT hand out first, then the LEFT, gripping very wide near the collars, then bends the elbows back. Grips are wide (about 1.5x shoulder width or wider) on these lifters. Duck under head down, settle, stand it up, 2-3 steps back (~1.5 s), stance a bit wider than shoulders, toes slightly out, then 6-10 s of standing still braced waiting for "squat" (cheeks puffed, chin level: V7 1:12-1:15). Five loaders: two at each sleeve and one behind; the side pair crouch down WITH the lifter, hands open under the sleeves, and rise with him.
- BENCH: stands at the foot or side, sometimes talks to the head spotter about the hand-off with gestures (V2 4:21-4:29, 5:32-5:41); straddles the bench and bends far down to set the feet (V1 4:11-4:14), sits, lies back, slides; low cameras show a huge arch with feet tucked far back and knees sharply bent (V7 2:54-3:00, 3:39-3:44). Head spotter lifts the bar out and steps clear; side spotters hover at the sleeves.
- DEADLIFT: most of these lifters pull SUMO (V1, V5, V6, V7; V2 and V3 conventional). Walk in, stand over the bar, often bend and touch it then stand back up (V1 7:06-7:08, V2 7:37-7:39), shake the arms out wide (V7 5:33, 6:49, 8:05), then a long "wedge": grip and bob the hips up and down several times, arms straight, 5-13 s (V7 6:50-7:03 ~13 s; V3 8:52-9:03 ~10 s).
- Attempt clock overlay (V2 3:19-3:27 counting down, V5 2:01-2:05 00:42->00:39, V5 2:50-2:55): lifters use most of the minute.

## 6. The lift itself

- Squat tempo: easy reps ~1-1.5 s down, ~1.5-2 s up (V1 2:20-2:24, V2 1:53-1:57). Grinders: 2.5-3 s up with a near-pause at the bottom (V3 1:46-1:51, V6 1:46-1:49). Depth clearly below parallel on all of them.
- Bench: ~1 s down, a visible pause on the chest ~0.5-1 s, press ~1-1.5 s. Grinders stall low and barely move for many seconds (V1 6:35-6:41 ~6 s, V4 4:07-4:27 "it was a fight").
- Deadlift: fast ones ~1-1.5 s floor to lockout (V1 7:29.5-7:30.5, V7 7:04-7:05); slow ones crawl the last few cm (V2 9:05-9:07). Lockout is HELD 1-3.5 s with shoulders back waiting for "down" (V6 4:59-5:02.5 ~3.5 s; V4 6:31.5-6:33 with his trademark head tilt). The bar is lowered with both hands on it, bending with it, never dropped on a good lift.
- Re-racks: squats walked forward into the hooks with the side spotters guiding the sleeves.
- Commands were never audible in these edits; referee hand signals only seen as an arm held out level, palm down, during a pull (V7 8:34-8:39) and a raised side-referee arm on the bench (V2 5:52-5:56).

## 7. Completing, the lights, misses and reactions

- Lights: Sheffield shows them as three ticks/squares in the lower third AND washes the whole stage GREEN (good) or RED (no lift) a beat after; the IPF stream shows three small dots. Lifters often do NOT look at the lights: most walk off and see the result later. The ones who do stand still and stare up at the board for 2-3 s (V3 6:39-6:40, V4 4:28-4:31, V4 3:26.5-3:27).
- Cards: a yellow card shown next to a red (V2 4:55); a card by mistake that was then corrected to three white (V4 3:27-3:29, he points back at the board laughing).
- Arguing a call: V2 5:01-5:07 she walks straight to the jury table and leans over it talking, holding up her wrist wrap; V7 3:10-3:16 he stands at the end of the bench staring at the side referee.
- Deadlift GRIP FAILURES at the very start: the lifter stands up and the bar never leaves the floor, arms flung out (V1 8:35, 10:21.5; V4 5:48). After it: looks at his hands, points at or complains about the bar, loaders brush the bar clean (V4 5:50-5:58).
- Failure at lockout: stuck just short with knees bent and shoulders forward for 2-4 s, then the bar comes down fast (V2 10:35-10:38.5). Worst case V7 8:22.5-8:27: the bar drops and he falls straight back onto the platform, lies flat with arms out for ~2 s, two loaders run in and pull him up by the arms.
- Bench miss: spotters step in and take the bar from the stalled lifter (V1 6:42-6:43); time-out re-rack (V3 5:18-5:36).
- Reactions to a miss are SMALL: flat face, hands at the belt, walk off (V1 3:43, 6:46); a hand on top of the head and an open-palm "argh" (V3 5:43.5-5:44.5); hand over the forehead (V4 4:33); a composed small smile and later both hands on top of the head backstage (V2 10:46, 11:05); an annoyed hop with one knee up (V6 5:33). No one threw anything or shouted at officials.

## 10. What surprised me / what a game would most likely get wrong

1. The pre-lift handler ritual at Sheffield is the EAR HOLD from behind (both hands on the sides of the head for 6-12 s, total stillness), not slaps or shouting. A game would invent face slaps. (V1 0:11, V3 2:04, V4 1:23, 6:04.)
2. The IPF Worlds coach ritual the user sent (V5 0:14-0:34, 3:20-3:22) is calm and functional: trap squeeze, gear fixed by a helper, CHALK RUBBED ON THE UPPER BACK, a pat/push on the back, coach stops at the edge. Lifter has AirPods in until the last moment.
3. The biggest celebrations happen OFF the platform at the exit with the coach/partner (V1 5:31, V4 2:12, V3 9:21), not on the bar.
4. A world record does not guarantee a celebration: V7 7:08 walks off silently after an open WR; V1 strides off grinning. Personality and stakes (winning, after a miss, emotional comeback) drive energy.
5. Men's top celebrations share a vocabulary: half-squat roar with fists low by the hips, double forearm flex, jump with knees tucked, high-knee stomps, pointing at the crowd, arms thrown wide palms up, hands on the top of the head, running off, bear hugs.
6. Lifters often walk off without looking at the lights; the broadcast tells the audience with a stage colour wash (green/red). A game should use the platform lights/colour, not make every lifter turn and stare.
7. Deadlift misses are often grip failures at the very start (bar never leaves the floor, arms fly up) or a lockout stall, and once a lifter FELL BACKWARDS flat on the platform (V7 8:22.5). Dropping the bar on a good lift never happens.
8. The setup is long: 4 s staring at the bar before gripping, a 5-13 s deadlift wedge with bobbing hips, 6-10 s of standing still under the squat bar. A game that rushes from walk-on to rep looks fake.
9. The corner BOW (hands on knees, head down, 1.5-4 s) before walking to the bar is very common at Sheffield, for men and the woman.
10. Loaders/spotters matter visually: five people, side pair crouching down with every squat, head spotter stepping clear after the hand-off, loaders rushing in the instant the bar touches the floor, and lifters thanking them with high-fives or handshakes.
