- Deadlift: stand 0.5-1 m behind the bar, shake out the legs, knee lifts or high-knee marching into place (opY 5:30, Hvi 6:23, 7:12, 8:08), bend and touch the bar then stand up again (opY 5:36-5:39, 6:40-6:46), hands on the belt, deep breaths, then a long final set (5-20 s). All women in my batch pulled SUMO, and so did the 105 kg man; the 74 kg, 93 kg and super-heavy men pulled conventional.

## 6. The lift itself
- Squat timing: descent about 1 s, bottom 0.5-1.5 s (one deliberate pause, Hvi 1:42-1:43.5), ascent 1-1.5 s when easy, 2-3 s on a grinder. On a grinder the hips rise first, the chest dips, and the bar nearly stops about 10 cm above parallel for about 2 s (NLG 1:39-1:41, opY 3:02-3:04.5).
- Bench: lowering about 1 s, a visible pause on the chest for the press command, press 1-2 s. The head referee's arm signals: arm held out horizontally over the lifter while waiting, a downward sweep for "start", a lift/flick for "press", a forward point for "rack" (NLG 3:25-3:39).
- Deadlift: 1-1.5 s when easy, 2-3 s on a grinder; the lockout is held 1-4 s, shoulders back, chin up (one man tilts his head sideways at lockout every time, opY 8:41.5); the bar is LOWERED with both hands until it lands, never dropped.
- Squat "rack": the head referee (blazer, at the front side) extends an arm; the lifter walks the bar forward into the hooks with the spotters' hands under the sleeves.

## 7. Lights, misses, reactions
- Lights show 1-5 s after racking (white checks / red X in the graphic; the floor light box with 3 lamps is visible at the stage front, opY 0:52).
- Misses seen: depth (2), a stalled squat the spotters took (XUC 3:02-3:07), a stalled bench the spotters lifted off (qzF 4:42.5-4:45), lost at the top of a deadlift (qzF 7:18.5-7:19), a deadlift that barely broke the floor (Hvi 8:27-8:28), a deadlift locked out and then a fall forward over the bar (NLG 8:20-8:21), and jury overturns of passed lifts (Hvi 1:05, NLG 2:54).
- Reactions to a miss are mostly CALM: a look back over the shoulder at the lights/scoreboard (very common), a puzzled frown, a stunned open-mouthed laugh (Hvi 4:43), prayer hands and a rueful smile (qzF 7:21.5), pointing thanks to the crowd (qzF 4:47.5), a blank face (XUC 3:08). Nobody threw or kicked anything or put their head in their hands in my batch.

## 8. Going back
- Lifters walk off the FRONT or the side of the stage, past the seated side referee and the floor lights, to an exit where a coach or staff member in a white tee waits with a hug, high-five, fist bump or bro-hug (nearly every attempt). Big lifts get long hugs (10 s, opY 7:17-7:26) or a jump-hug (opY 0:48); misses get an arm round the shoulders (Hvi 8:35).
- One lifter went back onto the platform to shake every spotter's hand (opY 8:52-8:55); another, withdrawing, shook hands with every official and spotter and waved (XUC 7:51-8:06).

## 9. The meet
- 3 referees (the head referee in a navy blazer at the front/side giving arm signals; side referees seated at the stage edge), a jury table with laptops at the front, 4-5 spotter/loaders in black tees and shorts (2 per side plus 1 centre/behind) and loaders with clipboards; a handheld cameraman sometimes stands on the platform (Hvi 7:09-7:11) and a camera crane swings over the crowd (XUC 2:45-2:50).
- Graphics: a lower-left lifter bar with name, flag, weight class and the 3 attempt boxes per lift (green = good, red with a minus = miss, white = pending), white checks / red X for lights; a top-right "CURRENT WORLD RECORD" box; an "IF SUCCESSFUL" rank-change box; a "FORECAST" projected-placings screen; an attempt clock bubble counting down from 1:00 near the platform (opY 1:23, XUC 4:14).
- Edit language: a gold "SHF" logo wipe, then a "REPLAY" slow-motion from a low angle (depth or bench), then a Powerlifting America shield wipe between attempts; three-star wipes.
- Lighting: dark hall, haze, a warm spotlight on the platform, coloured beams, green/red washes in the exit area. The crowd sits in tiered seats, stands for big attempts and is loud (screaming, chanting a name at opY 8:16, coaches yelling "COME ON, YOU GOT THIS, EASY WEIGHT" at Hvi 5:20).

## 10. What surprised me / what a game would most likely get wrong
1. The lifter comes onto the platform from BEHIND the rack/bar and faces the crowd; walking them in from the front or the side looks wrong (opY 2:36-2:38, Hvi 0:11-0:16).
2. Lifters do NOT celebrate until the lights; on a 2-1 they stay blank because the jury can still overturn (opY 3:09-3:14), and overturns really happen (Hvi 1:05, NLG 2:54).
3. The long squat wedge: 4-8 s bent forward under the bar with the head DOWN before standing up; a quick duck-and-stand looks fake (Hvi 0:26-0:34).
4. The head referee's arm signals on the bench (arm out, sweep down for start) are a strong visual and easy to animate (NLG 3:25-3:39).
5. The biggest men's celebrations chain: clap (chalk cloud), deep crouch roar with arms flexed down, a jump or punch, a roar straight at the front camera, then a long hug at the exit (opY 7:08-7:26, 8:45-8:50).
6. Backstage coaching is physical and loud: ammonia held from behind, trap kneading, shouting into the ear while walking beside the lifter.
7. Misses are mostly calm (a look back at the lights, a small shrug); constant anger would be wrong.
8. Women with long hair fix it with both hands behind the head before every attempt; deadlifters march high-knee into a sumo stance.
9. Bench lifters press down on the bar with both hands and talk to the hand-off spotter before lying down; this pre-bench ritual is almost universal.
10. The deadlift is always lowered under control with hands on the bar; dropping it from the top would be a game mistake (and a real no-lift).
