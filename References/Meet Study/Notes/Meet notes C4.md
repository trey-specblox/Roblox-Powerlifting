## 10. What surprised me / what a game would most likely get wrong
1. The coach is physically ON the lifter before every attempt (hands on traps/neck/ears 10-20 s, face to face, ammonia
   held by the coach). A game would show the lifter alone.
2. Lifters rarely wait for the lights: they walk off and the lights land mid-walk; one man celebrated a lift that was
   then red-lighted (c6JJZD1W-QA 8:30-8:34), another raised his fist and the red came under it (LxSRpjl_UGU 8:07).
3. Celebrations scale with the moment: openers ~nothing, records = big. Even a world-record squat got only a small wave
   from the showman (Mw95YfGN7Vg 1:56) while his third got a 7 s roar at the crowd.
4. The roar is done DOWN and FORWARD (half squat or forward lean, fists at the hips, chin out) as often as arms up.
5. The CLAP with a chalk cloud is a real, very common beat (before the walk-on, after a deadlift, as the opening of a
   celebration).
6. Misses are met calmly - talking to the judge, a hand on the head, a long stare at the screen - not rage.
7. Bench presses happen almost hidden behind two spotters leaning in; the hand-off and the spotters' cupped hands are
   part of the look.
8. The sumo deadlift setup can take 15+ s (hips high, then sinking, then slack pulling); conventional takes ~3 s. A
   game with one fixed setup time would look wrong for half the lifters.
9. Super-heavy body language is different: short shuffling walkout, head down, slow heavy walk, palms over the ears,
   arm swings before benching, and very little celebrating.
10. Each lifter has a SIGNATURE ritual repeated every attempt (wall lean, blindfold headband, palms on ears, arms up on
    entry). Giving each NPC one or two fixed rituals would read as real.
