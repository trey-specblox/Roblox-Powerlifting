  in the middle; behind it a huge LED wall showing the red HOUSTON logo with red/white light rays (or the lifter's
  name while they walk up). Two spotters (black tees, gloves), one at each end of the bar, standing still with arms
  down during setup, stepping in and bending toward the bar sleeves with hands ready (not touching) as the bar
  comes down and up (91:10-91:13), then guiding it into the hooks. A third loader stands by the plate rack (left).
  A side referee in a black blazer sits on a chair at the left of the platform (not standing). A man in a suit
  sits behind the head end of the bench (seen 91:49-92:06 behind the rack) - the head referee position.

## 10. What surprised me / what a game would most likely get wrong
1. Bench is not "lie down and press": the setup is long and the most animated part. 5-18 s lying under the bar
   (92:56-93:13 = 17 s; 104:20-104:38 ~18 s), re-gripping, wriggling the shoulders, lifting and re-setting the hips.
   A game that starts the press 1 s after lying down will look fake.
2. Women's arches are huge (100:53, 94:37) and several women use a LEGS-UP roll-back: lie down with both legs in the
   air, set the upper back, then bring the feet down far behind the knees (95:19-95:27, 112:04-112:06). They also
   use a leg swing to sit back UP after racking (109:46.5).
3. The "start" is a visible head-referee gesture: flat arm held out over the bench, then dropped (101:46-101:48.5),
   after a 2-9 s still pause at lockout. Then the bar comes down. "Rack" ~1-1.5 s after lockout.
4. Lights are never seen as a physical light tree in this broadcast; viewers get a 3-circle lower-third with
   coloured card bars and the reason text ("Elbow depth", "Downward movement", "Lifting position changed during lift")
   (95:44, 103:01, 114:29). Decisions can take up to ~18 s, long after the lifter has walked off.
5. Openers are quiet. 0 of 5 men and almost all women showed no celebration on round-1 openers; bigger reactions
   come on 3rd attempts and PBs (107:40, 108:39, 113:28, 115:42). A game that celebrates every good opener
   is wrong; energy should scale with attempt number / PB / placing.
6. Celebrations mostly happen WHILE WALKING OFF, not on the platform: stand up, turn to the exit, then fists or arms
   go up (108:39-108:40.5, 107:41). The finish is backstage: hugs, high-fives and smiling at the handheld cameras
   in front of the lit "GOOD LIFT" letters (107:42-107:44, 105:40, 106:44).
7. A miss can be laughed off: A3-26 (114:28-114:31) grinned and laughed walking off a 2-red miss. Not every miss is
   head-in-hands.
8. The walk-off is the same almost every time: lie ~1-2 s, sit up in one crunch, stand, walk to the camera-side exit
   while pulling the left wrist wrap open with the right hand (91:19, 118:19.5). Loaders swarm in to change plates
   while the lifter is still sitting (91:17).
9. Pre-walk ritual backstage is calm and private: headphones lifted off and handed to the coach, ponytail redone,
   palms rubbed at a pedestal chalk bowl, coach talking into the ear (91:32-91:36, 93:34-93:38, 117:40-117:43,
   119:15-119:18). Only some lifters hype the crowd on the walk-in (99:20 point, 108:00 double-fist V).
10. Presentation: the LED wall behind the bench becomes a huge name-and-photo card of the lifter during the walk-up,
   then "GOOD LIFT" after success; platform wash flashes green on good lifts, red on a miss. A game can copy this
   cheaply and it reads as "big meet" instantly.
