## Part 1. What each game does

### Sol's RNG: the best-documented rarity ladder (real detail)

You press Roll; a short spin plays, then the result's opening plays. **Every rarity step adds one new layer to the same base sequence**, so players learn to read what is coming before the reveal:

| Rarity (chance) | What the roller sees | What everyone else sees |
|---|---|---|
| Epic (1 in 1k to 10k) | Screen goes black with an ominous drone, then the screen edges glow in the aura's colour | A chat line: the player, the aura, and its "1 in N" chance |
| Unique (10k to 100k) | Black, then a drop-beat sound, then a 4-point star in the aura's colour, then the reveal | The same chat line plus yellow light lines flashing around the map |
| Legendary (100k to 1M) | As above plus a **white screen-cracking** sequence before the reveal | Cyan arches circling the map |
| Mythic (1M to 10M) | Coloured fog, an **8-point** star, a jingle, wind for the whole scene | Pink rings round the map |
| Exalted (10M to 100M) | Like Mythic with a heavier impact sound | Dark-blue rings and sparkles |
| Glorious (100M+) | **A unique cutscene per aura** (e.g. smoke, bubbles and a flashing anchor for a sea-themed one); a symbol pulses behind the star at the end | **Every player** gets a brief black-and-white screen, thunder and a giant beam, plus a second [GLOBAL] chat line |
| Transcendent (1B+) | Fully unique cutscenes with their own music | A custom world effect per aura (giant rings, rising particles) |

- **The star's colour is the aura's colour**, so it is an early, honest tell. Players react to the star before they see the aura.
- **Settings players use:** a rarity below which no cutscene plays; "always play the cutscene for an aura I've never had"; hide global messages; hide other players' chat lines below a chosen rarity; separate volume sliders for other players' auras and for server messages. (No setting found that hides others' map effects.)
- **Speed:** Auto Roll is free for the game's group members. **Quick Roll** (100 Robux) removes the animation; a roll then takes about 1 second.
- **What players love:** the freeze before a big one (people describe the game going still and then seeing the star), **fake-outs** (a scene that starts like a common one and turns into its rare version got the biggest reactions), and clipping the moment to share.
- **What players complained about:** some cutscenes feel low quality or have goofy sounds, some are **cluttered** with extra effects, and several **lack a proper ending**: the best-rated ones end on a surprise.

Sources: Sol's RNG wiki [Opening Cutscenes](https://sol-rng.fandom.com/wiki/Opening_Cutscenes), [Settings](https://sol-rng.fandom.com/wiki/Settings), [Rolling](https://sol-rng.fandom.com/wiki/Rolling), [Gamepasses](https://sol-rng.fandom.com/wiki/Gamepasses), threads [reactions](https://sol-rng.fandom.com/f/p/4400000000000104036) and [cutscene ratings](https://sol-rng.fandom.com/f/p/4400000000000078879).

### Pet Simulator 99 and Pet Simulator X (real detail on everything except the normal hatch's look)

- **Every pet can come out regular, Golden, Rainbow or Shiny**, rolled after the rarity: a second little surprise on top of the first. Shiny pets sparkle.
- **Huge and Titanic hatches are announced, not cutscened.** PSX put a [Global] line in chat naming the player and the "EXCLUSIVE Titanic" pet; after one update a Titanic was announced in **every server**. PS99's patch notes describe a rare hatch lighting up the whole server's chat. An hourly "Biggest Hatch" contest puts the winners' names across every server.
- **Gargantuan (the top tier) gets a cutscene that plays for everyone in the server:** the sky turns dark, the egg floats up into the sky, a crack opens in the sky, and the player rises to it and **rides the pet back down to the ground**. Golden/Rainbow/Shiny shows on the pet. All Gargantuans share the same cutscene.
- **Opening lots at once is the core of the game:** PSX sold Triple and then Octuple hatch (8 at once, 1,700 Robux); PS99 sells +15 and +40 eggs per hatch; auto-hatch exists (PSX 150 Robux). PSX's Egg Skip pass cut about 3 seconds off a hatch (so a normal hatch was at least that long). BIG Games later sped up the skip on purpose, while saying it shouldn't go too fast.
- **Odds are on the egg** (e.g. a Huge at 1 in 500,000). Robux eggs and luck passes exist.
- Players built a tool that auto-screenshots Huge hatches: the rare-hatch moment is something people want to keep.

Sources: BIG Games patch notes [Update 39](https://biggames.io/post/pet-simulator-99-update-39), [Update 60](https://biggames.io/post/pet-simulator-99-update-60), [Update 75](https://www.biggames.io/post/pet-simulator-99-update-75), [Update 79](https://biggames.io/post/pet-simulator-99-update-79), [PSX Cat World](https://www.biggames.io/post/pet-simulator-x-cat-world-update); [Gargantuan Pets wiki](https://pet-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Gargantuan_Pets_(Pet_Simulator_99)); [Rolimons gamepasses](https://www.rolimons.com/game/8737899170); [Try Hard Guides](https://tryhardguides.com/how-are-so-many-players-getting-hatching-titanics-in-pet-simulator-x/); [Dungeon Path](https://dungeonpath.com/posts/pet-simulator-99/huge-titanic-guide/).

### Blade Ball (detail on timing and skip)

- Crates stand in the lobby; you walk up and open them. They give sword skins and explosion effects.
- **The spin takes about 5 to 10 seconds and can't be cancelled.** The skip is **sold**: "Instant Spin" is a 199 Robux pass, and a 30-minute skip is a prize on the lobby wheel. One guide says it's only worth it if you open hundreds an hour.
- Normal crates cost coins, premium crates cost Robux. A "?" button shows the rates.

Sources: [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/what-does-instant-spin-do-blade-ball/), [Gamezebo](https://www.gamezebo.com/walkthroughs/blade-ball-instant-spin/), [RobloxDen](https://robloxden.com/game-codes/blade-ball/guides/blade-ball-how-to-open-crates), [The Nerd Stash](https://thenerdstash.com/how-to-use-case-keys-in-blade-ball).

### RIVALS (detail on features)

- Skin cases cost Robux (249, or 3 for 724) or season-pass tickets. An in-game odds viewer shows each item's chance.
- **Multi-open grew over time:** up to 8 at once (Aug 2024), then up to **100 at once** (May 2025). A new screen for viewing a box's contents came in Oct 2024. A top **Mythical** rarity with animated wraps was added for the game's most prestigious cosmetics.

Sources: [RIVALS update log](https://robloxrivals.miraheze.org/wiki/Updates), [Skins](https://robloxrivals.miraheze.org/wiki/Skins), [timesaver.gg](https://timesaver.gg/blog/roblox-rivals-best-skins-tier-list).

### Adopt Me! (summary only, but a useful warning)

- You buy an egg (Bucks or Robux) and then **look after it** (feed, bathe, play) until it hatches. That makes the hatch feel earned.
- In April 2026 a rarity-coloured aura animation was announced, pulled after feedback, and a version went live in August 2026 (rarity colours: white, green, blue, purple, orange for Legendary).
- **Player backlash, which matters for us:** the aura gave the rarity away too early, it felt slow, players wanted an off switch, and some found it too bright. One player said they'd stop hatching if handing over a pet took more than about 2 seconds.

Sources: [amvgg](https://amvgg.com/blog/new-update-adopt-me), [games.gg](https://games.gg/adopt-me/guides/adopt-me-rare-egg-hatching-guide/), [alphr](https://www.alphr.com/adopt-me-hatch-eggs/).

### Blox Fruits (summary only)

- A Gacha NPC sells one random fruit every 2 hours for in-game money, with a rolling animation (added in Update 27). **The wiki points out the fruit is given the moment you click**: players with low ping see it in their inventory before the spin starts, so the spin is just for show.
- A running joke blames the NPC's real-life likeness for bad fruits; a legendary result gives a title. No server-wide fruit announcement found.

Source: [Blox Fruit Gacha wiki](https://blox-fruits.fandom.com/wiki/Blox_Fruit_Gacha).

### Anime summon games (summary only)

- **Anime Vanguards:** 1x and 10x summon buttons; rates from Rare 75.5% down to Secret 0.004%; Shiny on top (1.5%); **pity** (guaranteed Legendary by 50 summons, Mythic by 400); auto-sell by rarity. **Every top-tier unit has its own summon animation** (videos on the wiki, not described in text).
- **All Star Tower Defense** has no skip setting, so players spam-click through 10x summons. **Anime Warriors** sells a skip pass (199 Robux). No global summon messages found.

Sources: [Anime Vanguards Summon wiki](https://animevanguards.fandom.com/wiki/Summon), [Pro Game Guides (ASTD)](https://progameguides.com/roblox/how-to-skip-the-summon-animation-in-roblox-all-star-tower-defense/), [Try Hard Guides (Anime Warriors)](https://tryhardguides.com/how-to-skip-summon-animations-in-anime-warriors/).

### Murder Mystery 2, Jailbreak, Arsenal, Bee Swarm (summary only)

- **MM2:** boxes cost 1,000 coins, 100 diamonds or a key; event boxes cost event currency. An official **Item Chances** button was added in November 2026. A Chroma looks like its plain version with a glow that one guide says is **easy to miss on a phone**. YouTube titles are all near-misses and "I opened 100 boxes".
- **Jailbreak:** safes bought with in-game cash, odds shown in the safe menu. A 10-second cooldown between openings was **removed after players criticised it**. Safe Keys exist so players in countries that ban safes can still take part.
- **Arsenal:** cases bought with in-game currency; the top surprise is an animated "Unusual" effect (2%).
- **Bee Swarm:** Royal Jelly rerolls a bee and **can make it worse**, which players call pure gambling; a Gifted result makes the result screen cycle through colours.

Sources: [MM2 Boxes wiki](https://murder-mystery-2.fandom.com/wiki/Boxes), [elyxir.gg](https://www.elyxir.gg/news/murder-mystery-2/murder-mystery-2-adds-item-chances-and-fixes-summer-event-confusion), [timesaver.gg Chroma](https://timesaver.gg/blog/how-to-get-chroma-in-mm2), [Jailbreak Safes wiki](https://jailbreak.fandom.com/wiki/Safes), [Arsenal Unusual Effects](https://arsenal.miraheze.org/wiki/Unusual_Effects), [Bee Swarm Royal Jelly](https://bee-swarm-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Royal_Jelly).

### The non-Roblox games they copy

- **CS:GO / CS2:** the server decides the item when you open; the reel is just a display, and the item is in your inventory before the reel ends. The gold "rare special item" tier has a gold background. A chat line tells the whole server what someone unboxed, famous enough that players fake it. ([Steam forum](https://steamcommunity.com/app/730/discussions/0/2946999778470321676), [win.gg](https://win.gg/news/heres-how-to-avoid-fake-vac-bans-and-knife-unboxings-in-csgo/))
- **Overwatch loot box** (the designers' own account): the box shakes and churns, light leaks from its cracks, it bursts open and four items fly up, each with a **coloured streak** for its rarity. The team first showed the rarity colours **too early** and moved them later, because the point is to build anticipation. Legendary guaranteed within 20 boxes. ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/why-opening-loot-boxes-feels-like-christmas-according-1793446800), [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/behind-the-addictive-psychology-and-seductive-art-of-loot-boxes), [Blizzard support](https://support.blizzard.com/article/000314267))

## Part 2. Patterns that work

1. **One base sequence, more layers per rarity.** Sol's RNG adds one layer per step: black screen, star, crack, fog, unique scene. Players learn to read the build-up, so the excitement starts before the reveal. The fwjelaa vault's reward note (in the user's kits) gives the same rule: each tier adds layers, it doesn't just get louder or longer. Keep the biggest channels (world going dark, UI hidden, server-wide effect) for the top tier only.
2. **An honest tell, late.** A colour cue just before the reveal (Sol's star, Overwatch's streaks) is the moment people clip. It must come from the real result, and it must come **late**: Overwatch moved it later, and Adopt Me's players complained it came too early.
3. **The top tier leaves the menu and becomes a scene.** PS99's Gargantuan leaves the egg screen entirely: the sky, a crack, the player riding the pet down. Sol's Glorious and Transcendent auras each get their own short film. A unique scene for the rarest thing is the pattern the user asked for.
4. **The rare moment is social.** A chat line naming the player, the item and its odds (Sol's, PSX, CS:GO). The biggest ones show something in the world to everyone (Sol's beam and rings).
5. **Players control the length.** Skip by tap, "cutscene only above this rarity", "always show it the first time", hide other players' messages. The games with the most opening (PS99, RIVALS) let players open many at once.
6. **A good ending.** Sol's players marked down cutscenes that just stop. The best end on a surprise or a punchline.
7. **A second little surprise on top.** PS99's Golden/Rainbow/Shiny, Anime Vanguards' Shiny, Arsenal's Unusual: after the rarity, one more roll for "special".
8. **The server decides first, the animation only shows it** (CS:GO, Blox Fruits). Ours already works this way (§35.12).

## Part 3. What to avoid

- **Long reveals you can't skip.** Blade Ball's 5 to 10 s spin can't be cancelled, so the skip became a thing to buy. Jailbreak's cooldown was removed after complaints. Adopt Me's players asked for an off switch. ASTD players spam-click through summons.
- **Screen clutter.** Sol's players called some cutscenes cluttered; the user dislikes clutter too ([[no-screen-clutter]]). A cutscene should be a clean film, not a pile of particles and text.
- **Forcing your moment onto everyone.** PS99's Gargantuan plays for the whole server. For us that would interrupt a lifter mid-attempt at a meet. Other players should get a quiet line and maybe a short glow in the world, never a forced cutscene.
- **Spoiling the result too early** (Adopt Me), and **too-bright flashes** (Adopt Me players had to look away). Keep full-screen flashes to one, and never more than three a second.
- **A top tier you can't tell apart on a phone** (MM2's Chroma). Legendary has to be unmistakable on a small screen.
- **Fake near misses.** Our reel already draws its other cards from the real odds and never fakes a near miss (§35.12). Keep it that way.
- **Goofy for the wrong reason.** Sol's players laughed at cutscenes that were cheap by accident. Our joke only works if everything is high quality and deadly serious, like a movie trailer. Cheap-looking gets laughed **at**, not **with**.
- **Spam announcements.** Announce only Legendary to the server. In a full server, Epic (4%) would come up too often to be news.

## Part 4. Roblox's rule for paid random items

From Roblox's official page, [Paid random items policy guidelines](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/monetization/paid-random-items) (read 2026-11-19; the page shows no last-updated date):

- **What it covers:** random items bought **directly or indirectly with Robux**: spin a wheel, hatch an egg, open a chest; random upgrades; combining items for a better result; and luck boosts, pity systems or rate-up items. **Indirect counts:** keys, re-roll tokens or tickets bought with Robux, and in-game currency that can be bought with Robux.
- **What must be shown:** **all possible outcomes and their real numerical odds, before the player spends** (for indirect purchases, before they spend the in-game currency). Odds as percentages that add up to exactly 100% (rounding allowed with a note). Shown in the 3D view, or behind a button labelled "Info" or "Details" (a bare (i) icon isn't enough).
- **Luck boosts:** must be explained in numbers before purchase, and the odds shown must update while a boost is active.
- **Restricted players:** check `PolicyService:GetPolicyInfoForPlayerAsync()`. If `ArePaidRandomItemsRestricted` is true, that player must not be able to use paid random items (Roblox lists alternatives such as a free earnable route, a guaranteed direct purchase, or hiding the item). If `IsPaidItemTradingAllowed` is false, they must not be able to trade the results.

**What it means for us today:** our crates cost **coins earned by playing**, and coins can't be bought with Robux, so the rule as written doesn't reach them (the page doesn't say this outright). We already show the odds anyway (§35.12). **This changes the moment any Robux reaches the crates:** coins sold for Robux, crate keys, a luck boost, or a Robux crate. Then every crate must list every rarity **and every look it can give**, with odds adding to 100%, behind a clear "Details" button before buying, and the PolicyService checks become required. A Robux "skip the animation" product isn't random, so it isn't covered (Blade Ball and Sol's RNG both sell one), but see 5.6 for why we shouldn't sell one.

## Part 5. Recommended design for our game (for the user to approve)

### 5.1 What already exists and stays

From §35.12: five crates (belts, wraps, singlets, sleeves, knee wraps), 500 coins each, odds 60 / 25 / 10 / 4 / 1. The server rolls and saves the piece **before** anything plays. Then a ~2 s intro: the crate drops on a lifting platform with a thud, rattles, the latches snap and the lid flies up, and a circle of light opens the reel. Then a CS:GO-style reel of gear cards slows over ~5.5 s and stops on the prize. One tap skips it. The rarity colours are grey, green, blue, purple and gold (`GearStatsConfig.Rarities`).

**Changing (user, 2026-11-19, GAME_DESIGN §40.3):** the opening becomes the player's own character lifting the crate up, putting it down and unlocking it, in the world, for every crate; a ~80 Robux pass skips this basic animation.

**How long it takes now:** ~7.5 s per crate. A crate costs about 2 meets of coins, so a player opens a few an hour at most. That's well inside the vault's guideline of under 10% of play time spent on reward animations. The length is fine; what's missing is **escalation**. Right now a Common and a Legendary end the same way.

### 5.2 One ladder: what changes per rarity

Each step adds one layer to the step below. All of it is a display only: the piece is already saved.

| Rarity (odds) | During the reel's last second | When the reel stops | Others see | Extra time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Common** (60%) | nothing | the card pops, soft thud, name and stats | nothing | ~0.5 s |
| **Uncommon** (25%) | nothing | plus a green colour wash behind the card and a brighter chime | nothing | ~0.8 s |
| **Rare** (10%) | the marker's ticks get louder | plus a blue burst and light rays (UI kit `Decor` rays) | nothing | ~1.2 s |
| **Epic** (4%) | the reel's light dims slightly | plus a purple glow round the screen edge (UIShadow), an "EPIC" banner, and the 3D piece turning in a viewport | nothing | ~2 s |
| **Legendary** (1%) | the reel **slows more than usual**, every light goes out except the gold card, and the crate on the platform starts to shake and glow at the seams | **the panel cracks and the game cuts to that crate's Legendary cutscene** (5.3) | the quiet news line at the top of the screen and in chat (the same style as records and suit drops), and a short gold column of light over the player at the Shop for anyone nearby | the cutscene |

Rules for the ladder:
- **The tell is honest and late.** The extra slow-down and the lights going out happen only when the piece really is Legendary, and only in the reel's last second or so.
- **The Legendary channels are reserved.** Nothing below Legendary hides the UI, cracks the screen or goes to the server.
- **Phones first.** The gold has to be unmistakable on a small screen: the whole screen goes dark, not just a glow on a card.
- **Sound for every step**, made with ElevenLabs and lined up to the animation ([[audio-made-with-elevenlabs]]), with powerlifting context in every prompt. The riser peaks on the reveal frame.
- **Reduced motion:** if `GuiService.ReducedMotionEnabled` is on, drop the shake and flashes and keep the colours, banner and sound.

**A second little surprise, later (optional):** a rare extra roll on top of the rarity (like PS99's Shiny), e.g. a "CHALKED" or "MEET-WORN" version of a look. Not now: it needs looks, and it would change the odds table, which is the user's call.

### 5.3 The Legendary cutscenes: the shape they share

The joke is that **unlocking a lifting belt is treated like the forging of a legendary sword in a fantasy epic**: slow motion, a choir, sparks, a deep trailer narrator, all of it played completely straight. Then it ends on something small and ordinary, and that contrast is the punchline (Sol's players reward a proper ending).

The shared shape, ~14 to 18 s the first time:

1. **The break (about 1 s):** the gold card cracks like glass, the screen goes black, a deep rumble. All UI disappears (letterbox from `MeetCinema`, the game's existing film look).
2. **The place (about 2 s):** a wide shot of an over-the-top location built for that item (a volcano forge, a mountain dojo...). A deadly serious narrator line (ElevenLabs, a real person's voice with real emotion, [[voices-real-emotion]]).
3. **The making (about 6 to 9 s):** 3 or 4 big moments, each on a music or choir hit. Slow motion on the biggest one.
4. **The handover (about 2 to 3 s):** the maker gives the piece to **the player's own character**, really held in their hands ([[scenes-make-physical-sense]]), and the player puts it on: the crisp belt snap for a belt ([[belt-snap-and-sound-context]]).
5. **The punchline and the card (about 2 s):** the music cuts dead, one small ordinary thing happens, then a clean card: rarity, the piece's name, its stats, the CENTRAL logo on the piece. Buttons appear last (a short delay so nobody taps through by accident).

Rules for every cutscene:
- **The first time, the full cinematic always plays** (user, §40.3), even with the skip pass. So the first time must be worth every second: keep it under about 18 s.
- **After that, the player chooses the length** (user, §40.3): e.g. Full / Short (~6 s: the break, the biggest moment, the card) / Off. A skip, where allowed, jumps to the final card, never past it.
- **It plays only on the opener's screen.** Their real character stays standing at the Shop with the gold light over it. Nobody else is interrupted.
- **Built so it can be reused:** one cutscene per crate (5 in total), not per look. The piece in the scene is whichever Legendary look was rolled. A special look like the duck floaty can have its own cutscene later.
- **CENTRAL** is the only brand anywhere: on the piece, the forge's anvil, the narrator's title card ([[gear-brand-central]]). No real brands, and nothing copied from a real film's scenes or music.
- **Legendary looks get VFX** (the user's plan, §35.12): the cutscene shows where that VFX comes from, e.g. a lava-forged belt that keeps glowing in its cracks.

## Part 6. Video study of the user's 10 videos (2026-11-19)

**What was watched:** all 10 videos the user sent, about 2 h 46 min in total. Three are Sol's RNG cutscene compilations (one in two halves), one is Hot's RNG (two halves), one is Pets Go (two halves), and five are short videos (Fisch x2, Pets Go huges, Anime Vanguards x2). Nine helpers worked in the browser.
- **Every second was gridded at 1 frame per second, back to back, with no gaps.**
- **The sound of every second was measured** while the video played silently in the page: loudness of the whole mix and of four bands (bass, low, mid, high). From that, the helpers detected hits, booms, silences, sudden cuts and risers. Claude can't hear, so this is measured sound, not listened-to sound.
- **0.25 s close-ups** cover every cutscene fully in B1, B2, B4 and most of B9. In B3, B5, B6, B7 and B8 they cover the key moments only (the transition into the scene, the main hit and the reveal); the many near-identical template cutscenes there got the 1 s grids plus their sound events. Each notes file lists its exact ranges.
- **Sound that couldn't be used:** a few stretches the uploaders muted, and one Anime Vanguards video with a song laid over it.
- About **530 cutscenes were logged**. The raw notes, with a timestamp on every observation, are in [[Unboxing Study Notes]] (`References/Unboxing Study Notes/`).

### 6.1 What the videos show

**Structure**
- **One skeleton for every rare reveal:** cut away, build-up, flash, return to the player wearing the item. Sol's, Hot's and Pets Go reuse one template dozens of times, swapping only the colour and the emblem. Keeping the opening identical is what makes the rare break-out a surprise.
- **Rarity buys length, a new place and camera moves, not more particles.**
  - Pets Go: a common roll ~1-2 s; 1/100M ~12 s in a plain egg room; 1/1B+ a themed world of 17-39 s; the very top 60-85 s journeys. The 62 s one drags.
  - Sol's: ~9 s templates, then 15-40 s mini-films at the top.
- **Best fake-out (Pets Go):** the rare starts exactly like a normal hatch, then breaks partway through: doors slam, an old-TV switch-off, a fireball smashes the lens. That's how the crate should "pull you in".

**Sound** (measured)
- **Silence before the big hit is the strongest trick by far.** 38 Pets Go eggs have 2.25-2.5 s of near-silence before the hit. Others use 0.5-3 s of dead silence, or "time stops" with the colour drained.
- Taking the bass away and bringing it back on the reveal also works: Endearment has 17 s with no bass, then about +48 dB on the white frame.
- The loudest moment always sits on the reveal frame. A bass boom lands on the cut-in. The highs climb 25-35 dB over 4-6 s into the flash.
- Dark scenes cut the highs completely (the Pets Go lava void).
- Cheap tension builders: a single crackle in the quiet, and accelerating heartbeats or ticks.

**VFX**
- **Aim for 3-4 out of 5:** one hero object, one colour family, a few dozen themed particles (embers, bubbles, leaves).
- 5/5 storms hide the item and get tiring. They only work for 1-2 s, followed by a clean image.
- Things that failed: white items on white bursts; near-black frames nobody can read; a lone symbol on black for 9 s; long dead white screens of 2.7-5 s.
- Sol's and Hot's show flash warnings: keep full-screen flashes to one or two.

**Camera**
- Most of these games barely move the camera: the object does the acting (slam, wobble, fly at the lens).
- The memorable shots use real camera work:
  - a low angle under a giant, with the tiny avatar below;
  - over-the-shoulder from behind the player facing a huge figure (Hot's Despair: Demon; Sol's Egg clip);
  - a rack focus with an object flying past the lens;
  - fly-through journeys;
  - a final orbit or low hero angle on the player back in the world.
- Letterbox bars give a cheap "movie" feel.

**Characters**
- **Show a giant in pieces first** (eyes in the dark, a fist, booms with one title word, e.g. Sol's Leviathan "KING"), then a shot where he doesn't fit in the frame.
- **The closest match to the user's buff lifter:** Anime Vanguards' gigantic muscular warrior towering behind the player's own avatar, ending on him flexing his back in flames for 2.5 s.
- Characters that speak to the player are the most memorable moments (Sol's Luminosity: "WELL, YOU FINALLY REACHED ME."). It's always typewriter text, landing in a quiet moment.
- **The player's avatar is almost never inside these cutscenes.** The user's design, where the player is pulled in, protected and thrown out wearing the belt, goes beyond every video studied.

**Humour**
- It works when played completely straight at full volume: a giant crying emoji to epic music, a fake blue screen, a tombstone engraved "RIP <username>", a turkey flying into the lens, a full build-up that reveals a tiny boat.
- Avoid fake "disconnected/kicked" boxes: they scare players.

**Afterwards**
- The best rares change the real world: an orange or greyscale grade, a giant object planted next to the player, a crater.
- A chat line names the player and the item.
- The unlock card:
  - a big themed title flying in tilted and settling in about 0.4 s on a bass hit;
  - the rarity word in its colour;
  - particles continuing over the card;
  - a "NEW!" sticker;
  - buttons last (Equip / Close / **Replay Cutscene**).
