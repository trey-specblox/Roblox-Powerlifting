# Celebration Catalogue

This file merges every celebration from the six clip notes ([[Clip notes A]] to [[Clip notes F]]) into one list the animator builds from. It covers 15 videos of real meets, studied on 2026-11-11. See [[Clip Study]] for the video list and the other topics.

**How to read an entry**
- **Moves** times are seconds from the start of the celebration (0.0), not video time. L/R means the lifter's own left/right. The breakdowns are copied from the notes. Where two notes saw the same moment, the best one is used and the other's differences are written in.
- **Sources** give the video id, the timestamp, the notes file and the entry number in that file. Lifters are described by country and singlet only. No real names are used in move names.
- **Sound:** the study was done with the sound off (muted frame grabs), so **no sound was heard**. Every sound idea is inferred from open mouths, claps and contact. Helpers B, C and D read some auto-captions, which showed commentators shouting on big lifts, the crowd cheering and, in one edit, background music.
- **Rarity:** Common (small, quick, low energy), Uncommon, Rare, Epic (big, full body) and Legendary (the most dramatic; gets VFX and a short camera cinematic).
- Entries run in rarity order, Legendary first. The free **default** (CEL-71) is last.

## What real meets show

- **Most good lifts get little or nothing.** In the full-meet and battle videos, a normal good lift ends with racking the bar, a nod or a fist bump with a spotter, and a walk off.
  - In the close -83 kg title fight, "most good lifts get NO platform celebration" ([[Clip notes B]]).
  - The -120 kg heavyweights did nothing on most first and second deadlifts ([[Clip notes C]]).
  - Three world-record setters at Sheffield were restrained: even world records got small gestures while walking off, not roars on the platform ([[Clip notes E]]).
  - One -69 kg lifter celebrated on the platform on none of her 9 attempts ([[Clip notes F]]).
  - The highlight reels (all three in batch A, plus 3i4jvmDrtdY in batch F) are hand-picked big moments, so they make celebrations look more common than they are.
- **Size follows the stakes.**
  - Openers and second attempts get something small, quick and private: a fist clench, a point, or nothing.
  - Last attempts that decide a medal or a title, big personal records (PRs) and records get long, multi-beat celebrations of 8-14 s.
  - Even a real title win can be calm: the -83 kg world champion clinched it with a smile and a handshake with his coach (CEL-69).
- **The order of beats is almost always the same:** bar down, then 0.3-1 s of stillness or straightening up (stunned), then one big move, then the walk off, then the coach.
  - The first 0.5 s is never a celebration: the lifter finishes the lift and sets the bar down first.
  - Many lifters **look over at the referee lights and only explode when they come on** (CEL-36, CEL-17, CEL-02, CEL-13, CEL-43). In the game, the big beat should trigger on the lights.
  - One lifter celebrated before the lights and then got two reds (c6JJZD1W-QA 8:30-8:35, [[Clip notes D]] C8).
- **Two beats feel best.** Quiet, then explosive (CEL-05: a bow, then a roar), or explosive, then quiet (CEL-04: a leap, then a bow).
- **Where it happens.**
  - Behind the bar, facing the front.
  - Walking off toward the exit and the coach, which is where the low handheld "exit camera" is.
  - At Sheffield, at the stage edge facing the crowd, or along the record wall on the way out.
  - The loaders are already stripping plates around the lifter. Nobody touches a lifter on the platform, except a spotter pulling a seated one up by the hand.
- **Body type changes the celebration.**
  - **Heavyweights plant their feet.** They don't jump, squat down or jog. They celebrate with the upper body (arms up, hands on head, a finger to the sky, arms out low) and a slow walk, and the big emotion moves to the coach hug at the exit ([[Clip notes C]]).
  - The super-heavyweight walk has a wide base and rocks from side to side, with the arms held away from the body by the lats and belly ([[Clip notes E]]).
  - Heavy lifters **can still go down to the floor on purpose**: a 380 kg super-heavyweight sits down and rolls onto his back (CEL-06), and a heavy woman drops onto all fours (CEL-12). They lower themselves; they never jump.
  - **Light lifters use the whole body**: juniors at -59 kg, women at -47 to -69 kg, and lean -74 kg men. They hop off the platform edge, jog off with heel kicks, sink into deep squats, tuck jump, prance and jump into hugs.
- **Lifters have personal signature moves** and repeat them on every good lift:
  - the HUN -83 lifter's "wingspan" (3 times);
  - a FRA lifter's bent-arm fist (2 times) and a GUY lifter's salute (2 times);
  - a USA -83 lifter's airplane arms on every walk-in;
  - a FRA -66 lifter's fist bump with every loader before each bench;
  - a LBN lifter's belt, arm-swing and chalk-clap ritual on every attempt.

  In the game, each lifter (players and computer lifters) could have one equipped signature move.
- **Coaches wait at the exit.** At IPF meets and Sheffield they never come onto the platform. They meet the lifter at the stairs or the backdrop exit with a handshake, a low five, a hug or a hand on the chest. Even the big coach moves (lifting the lifter, spinning her, catching a jump hug) happen there.
  - Exceptions: at a US team meet, teammates ran onto the platform (gfp-IdEaQNc 1:53).
  - At a US nationals, a coach knelt at the platform edge shouting cues during the lift (jfMBhKh4ab4 2:27).
- **Records change the room.**
  - Video walls flip to NEW / WORLD RECORD.
  - The stage or room light turns green on white lights (Sheffield 2025 and the US shows).
  - A French record stage fired CO2 jets and cold-spark fountains.
  - Lifters look up at the wall, which is a natural camera cue.
  - At IPF Worlds the light never changed, even for a world record ([[Clip notes B]]).

## Rules for every entry (R15 rig)

- **No face.** Every roar, scream, grin, crying face or "blank shock" in the notes is a face the rig cannot show, so **the body must carry it**:
  - **Roar:** head thrown back or jutting forward, chest out, shoulders up and tense, fists clenched, arms rigid. Hold the peak pose 0.3-0.5 s.
  - **Crying:** head down, a hand to the face, and shoulders heaving in a slow rhythm (rise and drop about 0.6-0.8 s each).
  - **Laughing:** quick small shoulder bounces with the head tipped back.
  - **Shock:** total stillness for 0.5-1 s with the arms hanging, then a slow move.
  - **Disbelief:** hands on the head and a slight lean back.
- **No fingers.** The R15 hand is one block.
  - A **pointed index finger** reads only from the arm line and the hand's tilt: the arm straight up, the hand angled in line with the forearm.
  - Fists, open palms and "fingers spread" all look nearly the same. Show them with wrist angle (palms forward or up = open and showing).
  - Interlaced fingers on the head become two hands stacked on the crown.
  - Prayer hands become the two hands pressed together in front of the mouth.
- **The floor and the root.**
  - Kneels, sits and lie-downs lower the LowerTorso through the Root joint while the HumanoidRootPart stays put. The physics state may need changing so the character doesn't fight back up. Mark these as **Floor**.
  - Jumps are also LowerTorso motion only.
  - The future body types (weight-class bodies) will change leg length and belly size, so floor-contact poses can sink into the floor or float above it. Check them on the smallest and biggest body.
  - Super-heavy bodies have bellies that hit the thighs in deep squats and kneels.
- **The bar is in the way.** After a deadlift the bar is on the floor right in front of the lifter, so a kneel must land behind it. After a squat the lifter steps forward out of the rack, or back from it.
- **Two-character moves.**
  - Hugs, clasps and catches need a matching animation on the coach or spotter rig and a set relative position.
  - A jump-hug catch is the hardest: it needs both rigs locked together for the carry.
- **Belt prop.** Taking the belt off means hiding the worn belt and attaching a belt model to the right hand.
- **Hair.** Dreadlocks or braids flying are accessories, not keyframes.

# Legendary

### CEL-01 · Record jump-and-roar with chalk burst
- **Sources:** c6JJZD1W-QA 9:42.5-10:02, slow-motion front replay 10:03-10:13 ([[Clip notes D]] C1). NOR -66 kg lifter.
- **Situation:** the last deadlift of the meet (317.5 kg), a record that wins the meet, right after a ~5 s grinder.
- **Moves:**
  - 0.0 lockout held, standing tall, shoulders back, chin level, sumo stance.
  - 0.5 bends the knees and hips and lowers the bar with control, hands on it all the way to the floor (bar down at 1.0).
  - 1.5 lets go and rises from the crouch; both arms swing out from the sides as he straightens.
  - 2.0-3.0 stands behind the bar, then steps backward and sideways away from it. Both arms are spread wide and LOW (about 30-45 deg below horizontal), elbows almost straight, palms open and turned forward, fingers spread. Chest pushed out, head up, mouth open (shout). Feet shoulder width, knees soft, facing the crowd side.
  - 3.5-5.0 walks toward the front camera holding the same open-arms pose, mouth wide, eyebrows up ("can you believe it"). The referee lights come on white at ~3.5.
  - 5.5 arms drop; walks a few steps.
  - 6.0 looks up at the LED wall where "RECORD" flashes. Face calm for ~2 s, as if reading it.
  - 8.5 both arms rise. 9.0 both hands land on top of his head (palms on the crown, elbows out wide): disbelief.
  - 9.5 knees start to bend, hands still on the head. 10.0 folds forward at the hips, hands over the back of the head, face down.
  - 10.5 straightens, arms come forward. 11.0 arms out in front at chest height, elbows bent, mouth wide (roar), small hop.
  - 11.3 skipping step, arms swing forward. 11.8 right knee driven up high, fists at the chest.
  - 12.0 **JUMP:** both feet leave the floor, knees tucked up, both fists punching forward at chest height, back slightly arched.
  - 12.3 lands, knees bending to absorb it.
  - 12.8 **big clap in front of the face: a large cloud of chalk bursts out.**
  - 13.0-13.3 right arm shoots straight up out of the chalk cloud, fist closed, head back; pumps it once.
  - 13.5 both hands back together in front of the chest. 14.0 arms drop, turns.
  - 14.5-19.5 walks off the back of the stage toward the exit, smiling, arms loose, shoulders relaxed.
- **Length:** ~14 s plus a ~5 s walk-off. That is over the 10 s target: for the build, shorten the 2 s screen-reading beat (6.0-8.0) to ~1 s, or let the cinematic cover it.
- **Energy:** 5. **Seen:** once (the biggest celebration in batch D).
- **Needs:**
  - a jump (12.0);
  - a chalk-cloud particle (12.8), backlit;
  - the screen wall showing RECORD (6.0);
  - the lights coming on (3.5).
  - Feet move a lot, so this suits light and middle bodies best.
- **Cinematic:** yes.
  - The source cuts at 9:47 (handheld front camera at platform level walking backward), 9:49.5 (the LED wall fills the frame with "RECORD"), 9:52.5 (side/behind handheld for the crouch and jump) and 9:55.3 (the chalk burst lit by a backlight).
  - Shot idea: side wide for the arm spread, then cut to a low front camera walking backward, then cut to the wall reveal over his shoulder, then a side-back shot for the jump and a slow-motion chalk burst against the backlight.
  - Dark hall, hot pink walls, white haze.
- **Sound idea:** two short shouts (2-5 s and at 11 s). A crowd swell when the lights come on and a second one at the RECORD reveal. A landing thud. A sharp, heavy clap with a soft powder "poof". The source edit laid music over it.

### CEL-02 · Disbelief, deep squat, arms to the sky, prayer hands
- **Sources:**
  - YLMRf70ALKo 13:01-13:12 live, replay 13:13-13:25, teaser 0:04-0:09 ([[Clip notes C]] C1).
  - OzNSQQoeHWo 6:01.0-6:12.5 ([[Clip notes F]] F5).
  - Both are a USA -59 kg junior with green-dyed hair, on the last, gold-winning sumo deadlift at the 2026 Worlds in Malta. The move order matches, so this is almost certainly the same moment from two edits. The notes disagree on some details (see "Notes that disagree").
- **Situation:** the last attempt that wins the title, a slow pull, and a 25 kg PB per the F captions.
- **Moves** (the C live take, 0.0 = bar touches the floor; F's differences in brackets):
  - 0.0 bar lowered under control; still crouched over it, hands just letting go.
  - 0.5 stands straight up, takes one step back from the bar with both feet, arms hanging, face blank (shock).
  - 1.0 right hand starts to rise toward the head.
  - 1.5 RIGHT hand grabs the top of the head (elbow out high to the side at shoulder height); left arm hangs; knees soft; head tipped a little down and to the left.
  - 2.0 BOTH hands on the head (right on top, left on the side/back), elbows flared wide, knees bent into a slight wide crouch (feet still at sumo width). [F: chest open, torso leaning back a little.]
  - 2.5 **ROAR:** both hands come off the head and are yanked down to the chest. Fists clenched in front of the chest and shoulders, forearms vertical, elbows bent and tucked, knees bent, head forward. [F saw no roar here: the hands slide down to the face and chest as he sinks into a half squat.]
  - 2.8-3.5 arms drop to the sides, fists loosely clenched, still shouting, stance wide, facing the front; small steps backward.
  - 4.0 **drops into a FULL DEEP SQUAT** (bum to heels, knees wide, heels down), fists clenched together in front of the chest, elbows tucked in. Lasts ~0.7 s. [F: hands over the face in the crouch.]
  - 4.5 rising out of the squat, hands still at the chest.
  - 5.0-6.0 standing, walks 2-3 steps (toward the loader area), arms loose, looking around and up at the crowd and the scoreboard. [F: stands still looking toward the judges and scoreboard, waiting for the lights.]
  - 6.5 "CAN YOU BELIEVE IT": both arms open out to the sides at shoulder height, elbows bent ~90 deg, palms open facing forward and up, head tilted slightly up.
  - 7.0 right hand back onto the head, left arm out to the side.
  - 7.5 **BOTH ARMS FULLY UP in a wide V overhead,** hands open, head tipped back (shout to the roof). [F: held ~1.3 s, knees slightly bent.]
  - 8.0 arms come down to shoulder height, palms up (a second shrug).
  - 8.5-9.5 **both hands pressed flat over the mouth and nose** (prayer hands over the face), elbows down and in, shoulders hunched, head slightly bowed; turns slowly toward the back of the platform. [F: then the prayer hands drop to the chin with a slight bow forward to the judges and crowd, and the right hand goes to the top of the head once more.]
  - 10.5 walks off toward the backstage side, back to the camera.
  - Loaders strip the plates on both ends the whole time; one walks right past him with a plate. Nobody touches him.
- **Length:** ~10.5 s. **Energy:** 5 (the C helper) / 4 (the F helper). It is jerky and emotional, switching between explosive moments and frozen disbelief. **Seen:** once (two edits).
- **Needs:**
  - body only, but a **full deep squat**: on big bodies the belly hits the thighs, so it suits light bodies;
  - a beat that waits for the lights (6.0) fits the lights trigger.
  - The rig has no face, so stillness and the hands carry the shock and the crying.
- **Cinematic:** yes.
  - The source held one front-left 3/4 camera, medium-wide, slightly above platform height, for ~10 s without cuts. The C helper suggests: hold on the disbelief, cut on the roar, go wide for the V arms, then a close-up for the hands over the face.
  - Bright, flat light.
- **Sound idea:** a short roar at 2.5 s and a longer one at 7.5 s. Crowd cheering through it. Plates clanking as the loaders strip them. The hands-over-face beat should go quieter (crowd under it).

### CEL-03 · World-record roar into wingspan and double biceps

  - 5.4-5.7 hands drop to the belt buckle.

### CEL-04 · Leap and bow to the platform

- **Sources:** YMrXwUrTpkw 3:39.5-3:45.6 ([[Clip notes F]] F7). USA -93 kg lifter. The same pattern after his third deadlift became CEL-11.
- **Situation:** his last bench attempt, a good lift.
- **Moves** (bench version; for squat and deadlift, start at 1.5):
  - 0.0 bar racked, still lying. 0.5 sits up straddling the bench, back to the camera. 1.0 stands and walks toward the front of the platform.
  - 1.5-2.1 walking, head turned to his left, **watching the judges' lights.**
  - 2.4 the lights come up; right arm begins to swing out.
  - 2.7 **BOTH ARMS FLUNG STRAIGHT OUT TO THE SIDES at shoulder height (a T),** fists clenched, chest pushed out, feet about shoulder width, knees soft, mouth wide open in a roar, square to the front camera.
  - 3.0-3.9 holds the wide T, arms bowing slightly forward, head tipping back, still roaring (~1.2 s).
  - 4.2 arms sweep down and in, elbows bent, fists at hip height; knees bend more (sinking).
  - 4.5 **CRUNCH:** both fists pulled in front of the belly, elbows out, shoulders rolled forward, half squat, roaring at the floor (a most-muscular-style squeeze).
  - 4.9 turns to his right and walks off, still shouting, arms loose.
  - 5.3-6.1 walks past the seated technical official.
- **Length:** ~4 s from the lights (2.4-6.1). **Energy:** 5. **Seen:** 2 times by this lifter.
- **Needs:** body only, feet planted, so it works at any size. Use the lights as the trigger (2.4).
- **Cinematic:** no. The F helper said "maybe". Shot idea if upgraded: a locked front mid shot at chest height with the lifter centred, then a cut to the exit-side camera.
- **Sound idea:** a big roar from 2.7 to 4.5 that peaks on the crunch. The crowd erupts on the lights.

  - It uses no big shape (no V, no T, no roar crouch, no floor, no jump), so every unlockable still looks special next to it.

**Default for every lifter (players and computer lifters) until crates exist: CEL-71, Chalk clap and fist pump.** It takes about 3 s and is mid energy (3).
- The feet stay planted until the walk-off, so it works at any body size.
- Its two beats (a chest-height clap, then one bent-arm fist pump) read from any camera on a blocky rig.
- It is exactly what real lifters do on ordinary good lifts.
- It avoids every "big" shape the unlockables are built on: no V, no T, no crouch roar, no floor, no jump, no prop. So it doesn't spoil them.
- Real meets show most good lifts get little or nothing. For computer lifters, consider mixing CEL-71 with a plain walk-off, keeping the full CEL-71 for third attempts and PRs.
