### How celebrations change with body type and stakes (what these two videos show)
- Light junior (-59 kg, YLMRf70ALKo): lots of whole-body movement: hops off the platform edge (5:00, 6:11), jogs off
  with the heel kicked up (6:12), bounces, and on the title-deciding pull drops into a full deep squat (13:05.5).
  Many small celebrations on ordinary good lifts (every successful attempt got some gesture).
- Heavyweights (-120 kg, oGfa7HoDrrA): feet stay planted; no jumps, no squats, no jogging. Celebrations are upper
  body only (arms up, hands on head, one finger to the sky, arms out low) plus a slow walk, and the big emotion is
  moved to the coach hug at the exit. Several good lifts got no celebration at all (DL1/DL2 of most lifters: just
  walk off). They look winded after a max pull: hands on hips, hands on the belt, unbuckling it as they walk.
- Stakes: opening and second attempts = small, quick, private (fist clench, point, nothing). Last attempt that
  decides a medal or title = the long, multi-beat celebration (C1: 10 s; C8: 5 s with a roar). A world record
  attempt that is MISSED gets a calm walk-off and a consoling hug (see section 8), not anger.
- Personality matters more than size in the pre-lift (see section 6): the big TTO lifters hype the crowd before the
  lift, the USA heavyweight spreads his arms and roars before the lift, the juniors stay inward.

  ~30 deg out from the sides with the palms open forward (12:42); walks to the bar (12:45); behind the bar both

  (12:52). Also earlier 8:51: same head-back, eyes-closed breath.
- "Touch the bar" (France junior, YLMRf70ALKo 11:30-11:32): stands looking down at the bar, then bends at the

- "Wings and V" (TTO black singlet, oGfa7HoDrrA 2:32-2:34 and 5:26): standing behind the bar he straightens up and

## 7. Setup and lift mechanics
### Squat (YLMRf70ALKo; USA junior, 230.5 kg world record at -59 kg, 2:03-2:15; France junior 0:15-0:31)
- Getting under: close-up 2:03: head ducked under the bar, face screwed up, both hands on the bar a bit wider than
  the shoulders, then stands it up.
- Walkout: 2 short steps back (France lifter 0:17-0:21); then stands tall and still with the bar waiting for the
  "squat" command (~1-2 s).
- Five spotter-loaders: one on each sleeve with the hands cupped under the bar end, one directly behind with the
  arms around (not touching) the lifter's torso, plus two more at the back. All of them squat down WITH the lifter
  and rise with him, hands hovering ~5-10 cm from the bar.
- Descent ~1.5-2 s (2:08.5-2:10), very deep (hip crease clearly below the knee), torso leaning forward ~45 deg at
  the bottom, knees forward and out. Ascent ~1.5-2 s with a visible slow-down just above parallel (2:10.5-2:11)
  on the record attempt; straight up to lockout by 2:12. Rack command, steps forward into the hooks (2:15).
### Bench (YLMRf70ALKo; USA junior 4:24-4:58, 7:16-7:26; France junior 7:50-8:00)
- Lies down from the side (4:28-4:30), plants the feet wide, then pulls into an arch by gripping the bar wide and
  driving the upper back into the bench (4:38-4:40, seen from the foot-end camera: wide grip, forearms vertical at
  the chest, big arch, head on the bench).
- Handoff: the centre spotter stands right over the lifter's head, lifts the bar out with him, and STAYS there
  during the rep with the hands hovering under the bar, which blocks the side camera (7:52-7:58).
- Rep timing: lockout wait ~1 s; descent ~2 s; pause on the chest ~1 s (start command); press ~2 s on an easier
  rep (4:52-4:56), 3-4 s grind on a hard one (7:22-7:26, bar crawling past the midpoint); hold for "rack".
- Getting up: the USA lifter sits straight up and turns to the side (4:58, 6:08); the France lifter grips the bar
  and pulls himself forward/up, sliding the head under the bar (6:41, 8:09).
### Deadlift
- Sumo (USA junior, YLMRf70ALKo 12:48-13:01): very wide stance, feet near the plates, toes turned out; hands inside
  the knees about shoulder width; hips start high (12:53), then DROP low with chest up (12:55-12:56) to wedge in;
  bar breaks the floor ~1 s later (12:57); slow through mid-shin (12:57.5), past the knees at 12:58 with the torso
  ~45 deg; lockout at ~13:00 (shoulders back, hips through). Floor to lockout 2.5-3 s on the max attempt. Holds
  ~1 s for the "down" command, then lowers under control (13:01.5).
- Conventional heavyweight (GBR, oGfa7HoDrrA 9:47-9:52): feet about hip width, hands just outside the shins, hips
  fairly high, back flat ~30 deg above horizontal; hips rise a little first as the bar breaks (9:47.5); bar above
  the knees at 9:48; lockout at 9:49 with the shoulders back, CHIN UP and head tilted back, mouth open; holds the
  lockout ~2 s; total pull ~1.5-2 s. The heavy TTO lifters lock out with the same head-up, mouth-open look.
- Grind (USA heavyweight sumo, 4:23-4:25.5): bar crawls from shin to knee with the back rounding and the head up.
- Missed pulls (LBN, 6:56-6:57 and 11:48-11:49): the bar comes off the floor to mid-shin and stops; he lowers it
  straight back down within ~1 s and stands up (not a stall at the knee).
- After the lift the two or three loaders rush in IMMEDIATELY (they start moving when the bar touches the floor),
  kneel at the plates and strip/change them while the lifter is still celebrating next to them.

  dropped onto her shoulder for ~1.5 s (7:03.2); then they walk off together.

  (11:50-11:52).
- USA junior third bench grind (YLMRf70ALKo 7:29-7:33): sits up with the head bowed, stands with the head down,
  walks off (result unclear).

## 10. Top picks (build first)
1. C1 "Disbelief hands-on-head, roar, drop to a squat, arms to the sky, hands over face" (YLMRf70ALKo 13:01-13:12):
   the richest, most emotional sequence; long and multi-beat; the obvious cinematic/legendary unlock.
2. C8 "Relief roar, then hands locked on the head" (oGfa7HoDrrA 7:40-7:46): a short, clear heavyweight version;
   works for any body type because the feet stay planted.
3. C9 "Slow walk-off, one finger to the sky" (oGfa7HoDrrA 5:42-5:44): calm, dignified, very readable silhouette;
   cheap to build (one arm, a walk cycle).
4. C7 "Hunched double fist pump while jogging off" (YLMRf70ALKo 11:53-11:58): distinctive introverted energy,
   different from all the arms-up ones.
5. C6 "Fist swing and jog-off" (YLMRf70ALKo 6:10-6:12): playful, bouncy; good common-tier exit.
6. C3 "Clap and point to the sky" (YLMRf70ALKo 9:10-9:13): short, clean, two clear beats.
7. C2 "Walk-off single fist flex" (YLMRf70ALKo 2:18): the minimal "cool" one; one pose held for a beat.
8. C10 "Low-arm strut into a coach bear hug" (oGfa7HoDrrA 9:52-9:59): if two-character coach scenes are built,
   this hug (and the consoling hug after a miss, section 8) are the best references.
- Also worth building as pre-lift unlockables: the "wings and V" ritual, the "raise the roof" crowd hype walk-on,
  and the "turn away, head back, breathe" ritual (section 5 and 6).
